# Preregistration draft: a within-student, concept-randomized trial of the learning loop

> **Status: draft, not registered (2026-11-08).** This is the analysis side of the direction audit's recommendation 6 ([`2026-11-07-direction-audit.md`](2026-11-07-direction-audit.md)). It becomes a preregistration only when four things are true: the owner and a partner institution's IRB have approved the design; the product work in §11 is built, above all the control arm, which does not exist; the parameters marked † have been re-estimated from pilot data; and the text is frozen on a public registry (OSF or REES) before the first student is enrolled. Writing it changed no product behaviour.

Companion artifacts, all on branch `feat/learning-trial-analysis`:

- `backend/learning_trial/`: the analysis code. It is stdlib-only and outside the request path; no application module imports it, and a test pins that. Run it as `python -m learning_trial analyze TABLE.csv`.
- `backend/scripts/export_learning_trial.py`: builds the de-identified long table from the database. It is a dry run by default.
- [`2026-11-08-trial-power-simulation.md`](2026-11-08-trial-power-simulation.md): the power report, generated by `python -m learning_trial power`.

## 1. Summary

| Item | Choice |
|---|---|
| Question | Does working a concept in Sapling's learning loop raise unassisted, delayed performance on that concept, compared with studying it as the student otherwise would? |
| Design | Within-student, concept-randomized. Each consenting student's trial concepts are split by a hash into loop instruction (treatment) and business-as-usual study (control). Every trial concept gets the same probe pretest and the same delayed post-tests. |
| Primary outcome | Correctness of the concept's 7-day post-test, answered unassisted (rung 0; spec §13 A112). |
| Primary analysis | Logistic regression of the post-test on arm + pretest + missing-pretest indicator. It is estimated as a GEE (independence) with a two-way (student × concept) cluster-robust covariance, a t reference and a two-sided α of 0.05. |
| Sample | 2–4 partnered courses in one term. With today's one-item post-test and 20 trial concepts per student, the audit's 120–200 students detect only 0.42–0.34 student-ability SD. Powering for 0.2 SD needs about 160–200 students with 40 trial concepts each and a three-item post-test (§8). |
| Not yet buildable | The control arm, which withholds loop instruction for a concept. It needs owner and IRB approval (§11). |

The headline from the power simulation is in §8. Under the assumptions there, a realistic effect of 0.1–0.2 SD is out of reach at 120–200 students with one post-test item per concept. The levers that close the gap are more concepts per student, more items per post-test and more students.

## 2. Background and rationale

The direction audit's view is that the pedagogy is sound but the measuring stick is weak. It recommends a preregistered within-student trial because a between-student RCT needs about 786 students for d = 0.2. Lindsey, Shroyer, Pashler & Mozer (2014) used a within-student, scheduling-randomized design of this kind and found that the gain persisted for a month. Kraft's (2020) benchmarks set the realistic size of the effect. The median effect on standardized outcomes is about 0.10 SD, and preregistered IES-funded trials have a median of 0.03 SD. The audit therefore treats a durable 0.1–0.2 SD effect as a strong result.

Two points from the audit shape this draft.

- **The outcome is not yet independent of the treatment.** The A112 post-test serves the concept's reserve item from the same generator pool as the loop's checks, and the same LLM grader family grades it. This draft keeps it as the primary outcome, because it is the outcome the product records today. It also makes an independent, instructor-vetted outcome bank and a blind human-scored subsample preconditions for launch (§5, §11). Without them, an effect could be an alignment artifact; Kraft finds that aligned measures inflate effects 2–4×.
- **Spillover biases the estimate toward zero.** Practising concept A can help with concept B. Trial concepts should therefore be weakly related, for example from different units with no prerequisite edge between them, or whole units should be randomized (§4).

## 3. Hypotheses

- **H1 (primary, confirmatory).** Among consenting students, a trial concept assigned to loop instruction has a higher probability of a correct, unassisted answer on its 7-day post-test than one assigned to business-as-usual study. The test is two-sided at α = 0.05 on β_arm of the primary model (§7.1). The estimand is the intention-to-treat, population-averaged log odds ratio, adjusted for the probe pretest, with the average marginal effect (risk difference) reported beside it.
- **H2 (secondary).** The effect is present at the 28-day post-test (persistence).
- **H3 (secondary).** The per-student paired difference (§7.3) has the same sign as H1.
- **Exploratory, labelled as such.**
  - Moderation by pretest (arm × pretest), as an expertise-reversal check.
  - Moderation by course.
  - Moderation by first-generation and low-prior-knowledge subgroups, only if consented data on them exist.
  - The "delayed" stratum, meaning no evidence for at least 7 days before the pose. That subset is selected after randomization (§6), so it is descriptive only.

H1 is the only confirmatory test. H2 and H3 are reported with 95% CIs and are not adjusted for multiplicity, and they are named as secondary wherever they appear.

## 4. Design

**Participants.** Undergraduates aged 18 or over in 2–4 partnered courses in one term, who give research consent (§10). There is no other eligibility screen. Minors are excluded.

**Unit and mechanism of assignment.** The unit is a (student, concept) pair. Assignment is `learning/arms.py::variant_for(user_id, node_id, label)`: the parity of the first byte of `sha256(f"{label}:{user_id}:{node_id}")`, which is a fair coin per pair. The trial label is set once, in `user_settings.loop_arm`, when the student enrols, and it never changes; changing it re-randomizes every concept. Because the hash is a pure function of ids, assignment is fixed before the student first touches the concept and cannot depend on anything observed. The exporter recomputes it. §11 asks for a durable assignment record as a cross-check.

**Counterbalancing.** Each concept is the treatment concept for about half of its students and the control concept for the other half, so concept difficulty is balanced across arms by design. The split within one student is binomial, not forced 50/50. A permuted within-student assignment (§11) would gain a little efficiency.

**Which concepts are randomized.** Only concepts on a per-course trial list are randomized. The list is chosen before enrolment, with the instructor, to be weakly related. In practice that means no prerequisite edge between them and preferably different units. Today's product randomizes every concept of a labelled student, so the list is product work (§11). Randomizing whole units, with one arm per unit per student, removes most spillover at the cost of fewer independent units. That is a design alternative the owner and partner should decide on. The analysis code handles it if `concept` is replaced by a unit id.

**Conditions.**

- *Treatment: loop instruction.* This is the product as it ships: probe, teaching on the H0–H6 ladder, graded checks, and FSRS reviews.
- *Control: business-as-usual.* The student studies the concept with their usual resources (lectures, readings, course materials, office hours). Sapling does not teach, check or schedule reviews on it during the trial window.
- *Both arms.* The probe pretest and the post-test windows run identically for both arms. After the concept's last window closes, or before the course's graded assessment on that unit if that comes first, the control concept is released to the loop. No student loses access to instruction before graded work.

**Blinding.** Students necessarily know which concepts the loop teaches. Outcome assessment is blind by construction: neither the post-test item, nor the grader, nor the long table's analysis carries the arm into a prompt. The analysis is fully scripted. The preregistered code is frozen, and the independent analyst runs it unchanged.

**Timeline.**

1. Enrolment in weeks 1–3.
2. Trial concepts may anchor (have their first graded evidence) until 5 weeks before the last day of classes, so that both windows can open in term.
3. The data lock is 28 days plus the 24-hour pose TTL after the last eligible anchor.
4. The export runs once, at the data lock, with `--as-of` set to the lock time (§7.6).

## 5. Outcomes

**Primary outcome.** The rung-0 correctness of the concept's 7-day post-test (spec §13 A112):

- The source is `posttest_poses.correct` where `window_days = 7` and `max_rung = 0`.
- The answer must come within the pose's 24-hour TTL (A89).
- There is one item per (student, concept): the concept's reserve item (A23, chosen clean-first, A113 e), never seen or revealed by the student before.
- The item is graded at H0 with no tutor, RAG or learner brief.

The window opens 7 days after the concept's anchor, which is its first graded evidence (the probe pretest when there is one). The 7-day window stays open until the 28-day window opens.

**Secondary outcomes.**

1. The 28-day post-test (H2).
2. The per-student paired difference (H3).
3. Both windows pooled with arm × window (§7.4).
4. Mechanism measures, each reported only descriptively by arm: unassisted next-opportunity success (spec §10), the hint rung needed, time in the loop, and the in-session check rate.

**Measurement preconditions** (none exists yet):

1. **Item review.** Every trial concept's post-test item is reviewed before launch and approved in the A113 queue (or vetted by an instructor). Better still, it comes from an instructor-written outcome bank kept out of the generator's pool and never served in tutoring.
2. **Human scoring.** A random subsample of at least 300 post-test answers, stratified by arm, is double-scored by two course experts blind to arm. We report human–human agreement, LLM–human QWK, MAE and signed bias, by arm. A difference in LLM–human disagreement between arms is the grader-circularity check the audit names, and a material difference is reported beside H1.
3. **Grader freeze.** The grader model and prompt are frozen for the trial and recorded (`grader_backend`, A116).

## 6. Exclusions and missing data

These rules are fixed before any outcome is seen. They act at the item level, so every student's outcome on an affected item is treated alike whatever its arm.

| Case | Treatment | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Item retired through A113's review path (`check_item_reviews.status = 'retired'`) | Outcome excluded | A112, A113 |
| Item with a student report not cleared by a later staff approval | Outcome excluded (S9 keeps it) | A113 (a, f) |
| Void pose: posed, unanswered past the 24-hour TTL ("expired") | Missing | A89 |
| Answered with help or no credit (`max_rung > 0`, including evidence journaled after the pose) | Missing (S8 counts it as incorrect) | A89, A93, A112 |
| Window opened, nothing posed (student away) | Missing | A112 |
| Pose answered before A112 recorded outcomes ("unrecorded") | Missing | A112 (d) |
| Concept without a usable probe pretest | Kept; pretest coded with a missing indicator | A112 |
| Student withdraws consent | All of their data removed from every export | §10 |

No student or concept is excluded after randomization for any other reason. In particular, the analysis is not restricted to the "delayed" stratum. In the treatment arm, the loop's own reviews shorten the time since the last evidence, so selecting on it would select on treatment.

**Attrition.** We report the share of due post-tests with an analyzable outcome, overall and by arm, for each window. `learning_trial.analysis.flow` and the exporter's dry run print these flows. The WWC attrition standard reads overall and differential attrition.

Student dropout is balanced by design, because every student has concepts in both arms. Post-test-level missingness can still differ by arm. For example, treatment concepts may be posed more often because the student is in the loop on them. The primary analysis uses all analyzable outcomes. It is valid if missingness is independent of the outcome given arm and pretest. Sensitivity analyses bound the departures from that (§7.5).

## 7. Analysis

### 7.1 Primary model

    logit P(post = 1) = β0 + β_arm·treatment + β_pre·pretest + β_miss·pretest_missing [+ window terms]

The model is fitted to the analyzable 7-day outcomes. With one window, the window term has a single level and drops out. It enters in §7.4 when windows are pooled.

**Estimation.** Logistic maximum likelihood, which is the GEE estimator with an independence working correlation.

**Variance.** Two-way cluster-robust, by student and by concept, following Cameron, Gelbach & Miller (2011): V = V_student + V_concept − V_student×concept. Each term carries its CR1 factor G/(G−1)·(n−1)/(n−p). If the sum is not positive semi-definite, its negative eigenvalues are set to zero; that fix can only raise variances.

**Test.** Wald t on β_arm, with min(#students, #concepts) − 1 degrees of freedom, two-sided, α = 0.05.

**Reported quantities.**

- β_arm and the odds ratio.
- The average marginal effect (risk difference), with a delta-method CI.
- The WWC Cox index, β_arm / 1.65.
- The SE of β_arm under each clustering, for transparency.

The implementation is `learning_trial.analysis.fit_primary`. Its coefficients, two-way SEs, t p-values and average marginal effect match statsmodels and numpy to machine precision on simulated data.

### 7.2 Why this estimator, and its limits

The trial's specified model is a crossed random-intercept logistic GLMM (§7.5, S1). The app environment carries no numpy, scipy or statsmodels, and this work does not add them to the app's requirements. The primary analysis is therefore its marginal analogue, implemented in the standard library. On the evidence below, it is the better confirmatory choice in its own right.

**Reasons to prefer it.**

1. **It is calibrated by the simulation that powers the trial.** Across 28 null cells of 1,000 synthetic trials each, the false-positive rate was 0.044–0.078, and 0.045–0.064 from 90 students up (§8).
2. **It is robust to treatment-effect heterogeneity.** A random-intercept-only GLMM is anti-conservative when the effect varies by student or concept (Barr, Levy, Scheepers & Tily 2013). The cluster-robust variance absorbs that variation.
3. **It loses little efficiency.** In 40 synthetic trials with 200 students and a 0.3 SD effect, the effect did not vary, so the GLMM was correctly specified. Even then, the ratio of estimate to empirical SD was 2.87 for this estimator and 2.97 for the GLMM (statsmodels' variational Bayes), about a 3% difference. The GLMM's reported posterior SD was 27% too small (0.060 against 0.083), while this estimator's SE was 7% conservative (0.079 against 0.074).
4. **It cannot fail to converge** in the way crossed GLMMs can.
5. **Its estimand is the population-averaged odds ratio,** which is the one WWC's Cox index standardizes.

**Limits.**

- The estimand is marginal, not subject-specific. The marginal log odds ratio is attenuated relative to the subject-specific δ; under §8's assumptions it averaged 0.65 δ. The test of zero is the same.
- The sandwich is a large-cluster approximation. At 60 students the simulated false-positive rate reached 0.078 in one cell; from 90 students up it stayed within about two Monte Carlo SEs of 0.05 (0.045–0.064). A bias-reduced (CR2) sandwich would tighten that, but it is not implemented.
- It gives no variance components, so the student-ability-SD effect size comes from the GLMM (S1).
- Like any available-case analysis, it assumes outcomes are missing at random given arm and pretest.

### 7.3 Secondary analyses (H2, H3)

- **S2:** the primary model on the 28-day outcomes.
- **S3 (H3):** for each student with outcomes in both arms, the mean correctness over treatment concepts minus the mean over control concepts.
  - The estimate is the mean over students, with a student-cluster percentile bootstrap CI (2,000 resamples of students, seed 20261108).
  - We also report d_av, the mean difference over the mean of the two per-student SDs (Lakens 2013), and d_z.
  - Implementation: `learning_trial.analysis.paired_difference`.

### 7.4 Pooled windows

**S4.** Both windows are pooled, with window and arm × window terms (`--windows 7,28 --arm-by-window`), so β_arm is the 7-day effect and β_arm + β_arm×28 is the 28-day effect. The two-way clustering covers the two outcomes of one (student, concept).

### 7.5 Sensitivity analyses

| Label | Analysis |

| S1 | The crossed random-intercept GLMM: `glmer(post ~ arm + pretest + pretest_missing + (1 \| student) + (1 \| concept), family = binomial, data = trial)` in lme4 (Laplace), run by the independent analyst. `learning_trial.glmm.fit_glmm` is the Python cross-check (`requirements-analysis.txt`); its variational posterior SDs are too small for inference (§7.2). S1 also gives the student-ability-SD effect, β_arm / σ̂_student. |

### 7.6 Data flow

1. The exporter runs once, at the data lock: `python scripts/export_learning_trial.py --arm-label <label> --treatment-variant <A|B> --as-of <lock> --out trial.csv`.
2. The analyst runs the frozen package: `python -m learning_trial analyze trial.csv --randomization 2000`, plus `--windows 28`, `--windows 7,28 --arm-by-window`, `--clustering student|concept` and `--course-effects` for the secondaries.
3. The primary result is the first table of that report.

## 8. Sample size and power

The full report is [`2026-11-08-trial-power-simulation.md`](2026-11-08-trial-power-simulation.md). It is deterministic and regenerated by `python -m learning_trial power`. The simulation analyses each synthetic trial with exactly the §7.1 analysis.

### 8.1 The effect-size scale matters more than any other assumption

"0.1–0.2 SD" is Kraft's scale: the SD of a student's score on a reliable test. For single binary items, the analogue is the student-ability SD on the logit scale, d = δ/σ_student, where δ is the subject-specific log odds ratio. That is how an IRT-scaled test reports an effect. The simulation specifies effects on this scale. Under the assumptions below, the same effects are much smaller on two other common scales:

- WWC's Cox index and the latent-total d both divide by the spread of one response, which includes the large item-level noise. On those scales the same effects are about 0.4 times as large.
- The research notes' arithmetic behind the audit's "120–200 students for 0.2–0.3 SD" standardized by the SD of a student's mean over a handful of concepts. That scale sits between the two.

The table gives the same effect on each scale.

| d (ability SD) | δ (log odds) | Risk difference at a 55% control rate | Cox d (WWC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 0.10 | +1.6 points | 0.039 |
| 0.15 | 0.15 | +2.4 points | 0.059 |
| 0.20 | 0.20 | +3.2 points | 0.078 |

So a Kraft-scale 0.2 SD effect is about a 3-point gain on one delayed item. Detecting a gain that small on binary items takes thousands of outcomes.

### 8.2 Assumptions (†: re-estimate from pilot data before registering)

- **Structure.** 3 courses of 40 concepts. Each student is post-tested on 20 of their course's concepts†, assigned by the `arms.py` hash.
- **Variance components (logit scale).**
  - Student SD 1.0† and concept SD 0.8†.
  - Course SD 0.3.
  - Student × concept prior knowledge SD 0.8†, shared by pretest and post-test.
  - Student × concept post-only variation SD 0.8†.
- **Effect heterogeneity.** The effect varies with SD 0.2 by student and 0.2 by concept.
- **Rates.** Marginal pretest rate 0.45; control post-test rate 0.55†; 20% of pretests unusable.
- **Missingness.** Missing post-tests are completely at random, 20–50%, half of it whole-student dropout.
- **Item variation.** Item variation within a concept enters only in the multi-item scenario, with SD 0.5.

These are deliberately ordinary values for educational item responses, not estimates from Sapling. The pilot or first weeks of the trial should replace them; §8.4 shows how much each one moves the answer.

### 8.3 Results (20 concepts per student)

The table gives the students needed for 80% power (two-sided α = 0.05). Each answer is interpolated between two simulated sample sizes and never extrapolated. Each cell has 1,000 synthetic trials up to 300 students and 400 beyond.

| d (ability SD) | 20% missing | 30% missing | 40% missing | 50% missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | ~2,590 | ~2,860 | > 3,200 | > 3,200 |
| 0.15 | ~980 | ~1,140 | ~1,330 | ~1,530 |
| 0.20 | ~480 | ~580 | ~700 | ~780 |

**Minimum detectable effect** (80% power, 30% of post-tests missing):

| Students | MDE (ability SD) | As a gain on a 55% base |
|---|---|---|
| 120 | 0.42 | +6.6 points |
| 200 | 0.34 | +5.4 points |
| 300 | 0.28 | +4.5 points |

Simulating directly at these extrapolated values gave a power of 0.77 (±0.02) at 120 students with d = 0.42, and 0.86 (±0.02) at 200 students with d = 0.34. The 200-student MDE is therefore, if anything, a little below 0.34.

**False-positive rate.** Across the 28 null cells of 1,000 trials each it was 0.044–0.078. From 90 students up it was 0.045–0.064, within about two Monte Carlo SEs of 0.05. The sensitivity scenarios' pooled rates were 0.049–0.055.

In plain terms: at 30% attrition, 200 students can detect about a 5-point gain on a 55% base rate. A 3-point gain (0.2 SD) takes about 580 students.

### 8.4 What moves the answer

The table changes one assumption from §8.2 at a time, except the "40 concepts and three items" row, which changes two. It shows the students needed for 80% power at d = 0.20 and the MDE at 200 students, both at 30% missing.

| Change from §8.2 | Students for 80% at d = 0.20 | MDE at 200 students |
|---|---|---|
| None (20 concepts per student) | ~580 | 0.34 |
| 10 concepts per student | ~1,240 | 0.44 |
| 30 concepts per student | ~430 | 0.28 |
| 40 concepts per student | ~280 | 0.24 |
| Three-item post-test per concept | ~320 | 0.26 |
| 40 concepts and a three-item post-test | ~160 | 0.18 |
| Student SD 0.7 | ~1,190 | 0.48 |
| Student SD 1.4 | ~320 | 0.24 |
| Control post-test rate 0.70 | ~650 | 0.37 |

- **Concepts per student is the strongest lever Sapling controls.** Power tracks the number of analyzable post-tests, roughly N × concepts × (1 − attrition). Going from 20 to 40 concepts roughly halves the students needed.
- **A three-item post-test is worth about as much as doubling the concepts.** It is also the instructor-written item bank §11 asks for on independence grounds.
- **The two together bring 0.2 SD within reach at about 160 students.**
- **The student-ability SD sets the scale.** The effect is d = δ/σ_student, so a more homogeneous class (σ = 0.7) needs about twice the students for the same d. The pilot has to estimate σ_student before N is fixed.
- **A higher base rate does not help.** At a 70% control rate, the same ability shift moves fewer answers from wrong to right.

### 8.5 Planned sample size

1. **The audit's sizing does not hold on Kraft's scale.** With today's one-item post-test and about 20 trial concepts each, 120–200 students detect only 0.42–0.34 ability SD, about a 5–7-point gain. That falls in what Kraft calls a large effect. The audit's "0.2–0.3 SD" figure comes from a different standardization (§8.1).
2. **A 0.2 SD target is reachable** if the §11 product work is built:
   - 40 trial concepts per student;
   - an instructor-written three-item post-test per concept and window;
   - about 160–200 consenting students, the upper end if more than 30% of post-tests go missing.

   Without the item bank, 40 concepts need about 280 students.
3. **0.1 SD is out of reach for a one-term trial at this scale.** It needs about 2,900 students with today's design. Preregister 0.2 SD as the effect the trial is powered for, and report the 95% CI whatever the result. A null at that size rules out large effects; it does not show that the loop does nothing.
4. **Fix N only after the pilot.** Re-run `python -m learning_trial power` with the pilot's estimates in `simulate.Design`:
   - σ_student and σ_concept from S1;
   - the pretest–post-test association;
   - the realized concepts per student and attrition.

   Register the resulting N and MDE, not this table's.

## 9. Stopping rule

The trial has a fixed sample and a single analysis.

- **Enrolment.** It closes at the end of week 3 or when the planned N have consented, whichever comes first.
- **Data collection.** It ends at the data lock (§4).
- **Interim looks.** There are no interim analyses of outcomes by arm, and no stopping for efficacy or futility. The trial is never stopped or extended because of how the results look.

**Arm-blind monitoring.** Weekly, the operator reviews:

- enrolment;
- the share of due post-tests answered (overall only);
- pose expiry;
- item retirements and reports;
- the zero-reveal gate (spec §10, A88).

**Integrity stop.** The trial pauses, and the pause is logged as a deviation, if any of these happens:

- a trial post-test item is served in tutoring;
- a solution reveal breaks the zero-leak gate;
- a control concept receives loop instruction at more than a trivial rate;
- the grader model changes.

It resumes only when the cause is fixed. Concepts affected while the cause was active are flagged in a prespecified sensitivity analysis.

**Harm stop.** The trial runs only on ungraded practice, and control concepts are released before graded work, so there is no outcome-based harm rule. An IRB may require one. If it does, the IRB names an independent monitor and a Haybittle–Peto boundary (p < 0.001), which leaves the final α essentially at 0.05.

## 10. Consent, IRB and privacy

**IRB.** The study runs through a faculty partner's IRB. Comparing normal practice with a supplement on ungraded material plausibly fits the Common Rule exemption for normal educational practices, 45 CFR 46.104(d)(1). That exemption requires that the practice is "not likely to adversely impact students' opportunity to learn required educational content". The release of control concepts before graded work (§4) is how the design meets that condition. The decision belongs to the IRB, not Sapling. Without a partner, an independent commercial IRB is the fallback.

**Consent.** Research consent is opt-in and separate from the terms of service. It states:

- that the concepts in the student's courses are randomly split between Sapling teaching and their usual study, and that untaught concepts are released later;
- that short unassisted post-tests follow at 7 and 28 days;
- what is recorded, and that the analysis file carries no names or text;
- that participation and withdrawal have no effect on grades or access;
- that the student may withdraw at any time, after which their data are removed from every later export.

Consent is recorded with its version and time; that record is not built (§11). Setting `user_settings.loop_arm` to the trial label is the enrolment act, so it happens only after recorded consent. Withdrawal clears the label.

**FERPA.** Course exam items or grades, if they are ever used as outcomes, require a FERPA-compliant release, or de-identified data supplied by the institution.

**De-identification.** Every export's ids are salted HMAC-SHA256 pseudonyms (`learning_trial.deidentify`). The salt is drawn fresh for each export and never written or printed, so an export can be joined neither to another export nor back to the database.

- The export carries no concept names, item text, answers or timestamps: only arms, windows, booleans, enums, a rounded delay and pseudonyms.
- Rows are sorted by pseudonym.
- Tests pin that no raw id or text leaves.

The analysis file goes to the independent analyst, and its retention period is set by the IRB protocol.

**Incentives.** Any payment for completing post-tests is the same for every student. Arms are within-student, so incentives cannot differ by arm.

**Risks.** The main risk is less instruction on control concepts during the window. It is mitigated by business-as-usual access, release before graded work and trial lists of ungraded practice concepts. The loop's own risks (answer leaks, wrong keys) are those of the product and are covered by its gates and the A113 item guards.

## 11. What the product must still build (described, not built)

The control arm is not built, and building it needs owner and IRB approval. Today `learning/arms.py`'s variant B changes only the genuine-attempt gate's independent-time threshold for the develop and proficient bands (`GATE_INDEPENDENT_MIN_S_VARIANT_B`, A6). A labelled student's sessions are also exempt from tier downgrades (spec §10). Both variants receive full loop instruction, so the current A/B plumbing can compare loop policies but cannot compare the loop with business-as-usual.

1. **A control arm that withholds loop instruction for a concept.**
   - *Behaviour.* For a trial-labelled student, a (student, concept) assigned to control gets the probe pretest and the post-test windows and nothing else from the loop until release. That means no teaching turns, no in-session checks and no FSRS reviews. The concept is skipped when a session plans its steps.
   - *Decisions needed.*
     - Whether Sapling's free tutor chat also declines to teach a control concept during the window. The clean contrast requires that it does.
     - What the student is told, for example "this topic is part of a study; Sapling will start teaching it on <date>".
     - The release rule.
   - *Spec work.* A spec §13 amendment and invariant updates: a control concept writes no evidence except the probe and the post-test.
2. **A consent record and an enrolment path.** A durable consent table holds the study, consent version, consented-at and withdrawn-at times. An admin action sets `loop_arm` only for consented students and clears it on withdrawal.
3. **A pretest for every trial concept in both arms.** The concept's first contact must be an independent probe grade on a fresh item, so that A112's pretest definition holds for control concepts too.
4. **A durable assignment record.** An append-only record of (student, node, label, variant, assigned_at), written when a trial concept is first touched. The export then cross-checks it rather than relying on the label alone.
5. **Per-course trial concept lists** (weakly related concepts or whole units). Only listed concepts are randomized; the rest get the loop as usual.
6. **Independent outcome items.** An instructor-vetted post-test bank per trial concept, outside the generator's pool and never served in tutoring, made of selected-response or human-scored items. Ideally there are 2–3 items per concept per window; the simulation shows that three items per post-test is worth about as much as doubling the number of concepts (§8.4).
7. **A blind human-scoring workflow** for the §5 subsample.
8. **Post-test reach.** Window-open reminders sent identically to both arms. Attrition is the cheapest power to buy back (§8.3).
9. **Optionally, balanced within-student assignment.** Each student's trial concepts are ranked by the hash and the top half assigned, instead of using parity.

## 12. Transparency and deviations

- Register this document, the frozen `learning_trial` code (a commit hash) and the synthetic power report before enrolment.
- Report every preregistered outcome, including nulls, with the flow table.
- An independent analyst runs the frozen code.
- Deviations are logged with dates and reasons, and reported.
- The code and synthetic data are public; the trial data are shared only if consent allows.

## Appendix A. The long table

One row per (student, concept, opened window). The columns are documented in `backend/learning_trial/table.py`:

- `student`, `course`, `concept`: pseudonyms. `concept` is course-level, so students in one course share it.
- `arm`: treatment or control.
- `window`: 7 or 28.
- `pretest`, `posttest`: 1, 0 or empty.
- `status`: rung0, assisted, unrecorded, open, expired or not_posed.
- `exclusion`: retired_item, reported_item or empty.
- `item`: pseudonym of the post-test item.
- `days_since_evidence`: descriptive only. It is measured after randomization, so it never enters a model.
- Optional numeric `cov_*` columns for pre-randomization covariates.
