# Anchor Sapling's tutor in graded, weighted evidence

The best-evidenced design for Sapling is not a smarter chat prompt but a loop in which **only graded checks move a per-concept knowledge estimate**, that estimate decays with time, and a scheduler brings each concept back for retrieval when it is near forgetting. Concretely: replace today's scalar mastery score (moved by the tutor's own judgment between −0.1 and +0.3, and by ±0.03/−0.02 per quiz item) with **Bayesian Knowledge Tracing run with fixed, bounded parameters and a separate guess/slip pair per evidence channel** (free response, multiple choice, teach-back, chat turn), decayed toward the prior at read time by an FSRS-6 retrievability curve, and propagated one hop along prerequisite edges; declare "proficient" at P(known) ≥ 0.95 and "mastered" at ≥ 0.98 with at least three strong-channel observations. Run each session as probe (4–6 adaptive items per skill, aimed at 50–70% expected success, stop at SE ≈ 0.3 or a floor hit) → plan over the prerequisite "outer fringe" → one step of ≤ 3–5 sentences ending in a student action → a check → task/process/self-regulation feedback that never ends in the answer → a written close with an if-then plan for next time. Schedule review with FSRS-6 at a 0.90 retention target and a successive-relearning rule of three correct recalls then three spaced relearns; interleave sibling concepts only after acquisition. The guardrail evidence is unambiguous: the identical model class produced **−17% on an unassisted exam when it gave answers (Bastani 2025, n≈1,000, classroom RCT) and +0.73–1.3 SD when it was forced to scaffold one part at a time against instructor-authored solutions (Kestin 2025, n=194, classroom RCT)**, so answer-withholding, grounding in verified solutions, and a deterministic sequencer are load-bearing, not polish. Learning must be measured on tool-removed, delayed checks; in-session accuracy is inflated 48–127% by assistance and can move in the opposite direction from retention. The "How I Use AI to Learn Things" system got the loop skeleton right (probe → plan → teach, one reasoning step at a time, multiple choice as a measurement instrument) and got the evidence layer wrong (no spaced review, no free-response or teach-back checks, no grounding, no memory, no outcome measure); its commenters supplied most of the fixes, and the literature supports nearly all of them. Realistic expectations: single well-implemented techniques deliver **d ≈ 0.3–0.6 on course-aligned measures** and far less on standardized tests; nothing replicates at Bloom's 2 SD.

## What works and at what size: 0.2 to 0.8 SD, never 2

The stakes for Sapling's design are set by two decades of intelligent-tutoring evidence and two 2025 randomized trials of LLM tutors. Step-based tutoring — feedback and hints on each step of a multi-step problem, with mastery-based problem selection — is the one architecture with replicated evidence: VanLehn's 2011 review put **intelligent tutoring systems at d = 0.76 versus no tutoring, essentially equal to human tutors at d = 0.79**, while substep systems that micromanage every fragment of reasoning fell to 0.40 ([VanLehn 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ946764); [Alkhatlan & Kalita survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.09628)). At school scale the number shrinks: Cognitive Tutor Algebra I produced nothing in year one and ≈ 0.2 SD in year two of a RAND school-level RCT ([Pane et al. 2014](https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP50410.html)), and ASSISTments homework support gave g = 0.18 in Maine ([Roschelle et al. 2016](https://www.sri.com/publication/education-learning-pubs/digital-learning-pubs/how-big-is-that-reporting-the-effect-size-and-cost-of-assistments-in-the-maine-homework-efficacy-study/)). Bloom's famous 2 SD came from small dissertation studies that held tutees to a 90% mastery criterion while classroom controls needed 80% and advanced regardless; Kulik's 1990 meta-analysis of 108 mastery-learning evaluations gives d = 0.52 on local tests and **0.08 on standardized tests** ([Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns 1990](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543060002265); [Nintil review](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/)). The actionable lesson from Bloom is the mechanism that inflated his number: do not advance a student who has not demonstrated mastery.

The LLM-era results converge on one pattern. In Bastani et al.'s PNAS 2025 field experiment (~1,000 Turkish high-school students, four 90-minute math sessions), vanilla GPT-4 access raised practice scores +48% and then **lowered the unassisted exam by 17% (coef −0.054, p<0.05)**; a "GPT Tutor" with teacher-authored solutions and hint-not-answer prompting raised practice +127% and left the exam at control level (coef −0.004, n.s.) — harm removed, no positive effect ([Bastani et al. 2025, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)). Secondary reports express the same contrast as ≈ −0.19 SD versus −0.01 SD ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/)). Kestin et al.'s Harvard crossover RCT (n = 194, intro physics) had a GPT-4 tutor with brevity, no-full-solution and encouragement instructions, platform-enforced sequencing through each problem part, and embedded expert solutions; the AI condition's **median post-test was 4.5 versus 3.5 for active-learning class, an effect of 0.73–1.3 SD by quantile regression, in less time** ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6); [ETCJ review](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/)). Tutor CoPilot, the first RCT of human-AI tutoring (900 tutors, 1,800 students), lifted topic mastery +4 pp overall and +9 pp for students of lower-rated tutors, because tutors asked more guiding questions and gave fewer answers ([Wang, Demszky et al. 2024](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-1054)). A World Bank RCT in Nigeria (~800 students, six weeks, teacher-orchestrated Copilot sessions) found +0.31 SD overall and +0.23 SD in English, with dose-response ([De Simone et al. 2025](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099548105192529324)). The one independent Khanmigo study (69 undergraduates, concept inventory) found no difference between Khanmigo, Google search and paper — a null that also shows how underpowered small pre/post designs are ([JTL 2025](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1487444)). Treat Kestin's ~1 SD as an upper bound from a one-week, expert-authored unit with an immediate post-test; the ITS history says at-scale effects run 3–4× smaller than lab effects.

The rest of the evidence base sorts cleanly by tier. Classroom-replicated and large: retrieval practice g = 0.50–0.61 across 217 studies ([Adesope et al. 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654316689306)) and g = 0.499 across 222 classroom quizzing studies, rising to 0.537 with corrective feedback ([Yang et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33683913/)); high-information feedback d = 0.99 against an overall feedback d = 0.48 with 17% of effects negative ([Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987456/)); self-explanation g = 0.55 ([Bisra et al. 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x)); undergraduate active learning +0.47 SD with failure odds 1.95× under lecture ([Freeman et al. 2014](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.1319030111)); expertise reversal, with assistance helping novices d = 0.505 and hurting experts d = −0.428 across 60 experiments ([Tetzlaff et al. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475225000660)); personalized spaced review d = 1.42 on a 28-day-delayed exam ([Lindsey et al. 2014](https://library.scottbarrykaufman.com/uploads/2014/01/Lindsey-et-al.-2014.pdf)). Moderate with boundary conditions: learning-by-teaching g = 0.35 preparing and 0.56 actually teaching ([Kobayashi 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jpr.12221)); productive failure g = 0.36 that reverses for grades 2–5 ([Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105)); interleaving g = 0.42, driven by confusable material ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)); gamification g = 0.49 cognitive but 0.36 motivational and 0.25 behavioral ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270)). Model-only: the 85% rule ([Wilson et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12552-4)). Vendor-only: Khan Academy's +6.1% next-item correctness ([Khan blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)), LearnLM's 71–82% educator preference ([arXiv 2505.24477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24477)), Duolingo's +15% completion ([Duolingo](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-max-shows-future-ai-education)). Rater-preference only, with no learning-outcome RCT: Socratic-only style, growth-mindset phrasing, metacognitive prompts as isolated treatments, RAG citations, Study Mode, Learning Mode. Everything Sapling does below sits on the first two tiers wherever possible and is labeled where it does not.

## Replace the scalar mastery score with per-channel BKT plus decay

Sapling today has one number per concept that the tutor model nudges by its own judgment and the quiz nudges by a fixed ±0.03/−0.02 regardless of length or difficulty, with no decay and tier cuts at 0.1/0.45/0.75. The learner-modeling literature gives a clear answer on what should replace it, and the answer is driven by Sapling's data regime — tens of students per course, a few observations per concept — rather than by leaderboard accuracy.

The benchmark story first, because it removes deep knowledge tracing from consideration. Piech's 2015 claim that DKT beat BKT by 25% AUC collapsed on re-analysis: **31.6% of the gap was a biased AUC computation and another 50.6% vanished once BKT was given a forgetting parameter**, after which BKT+Forgetting scored 0.83 versus DKT's 0.86 and BKT with skill discovery reached 0.90 ([Khajah, Lindsey & Mozer 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.02416)). Knewton could not reproduce DKT's 0.86 at all and found plain IRT beating it after de-duplicating the log (0.765 vs 0.743) ([Wilson et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02336)). Gervet's nine-dataset benchmark found logistic regression with good features leads on moderate data, DKT leads only on very large sets, and "DKT overfits small datasets"; the smallest set where anything neural was competitive still had 574 learners ([Gervet et al. 2020](https://theophilegervet.github.io/assets/pdf/gervet2020deep.pdf)). On 153 tutoring dialogues, BKT, DKT, DKVMN, AKT, SAINT and simpleKT all scored AUC 0.47–0.53, no better than majority class ([Scarlatos, Baker & Lan 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16490)). Fitting even BKT wants roughly **50 students and 15 opportunities per skill** for parameters to converge ([Badrinath, Wang & Pardos 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.00385v2)). Pelánek's practitioner conclusion — "it is thus preferable to use simple learner models unless there is a clear reason to prefer more complex models," and for mastery decisions "it is more important what data are used for modeling than the exact details of models" — is the governing principle ([Pelánek 2017](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/umuai-overview.pdf)). A specialized KT model also costs under $2/year to serve 100,000 students, so the learner model must never be an LLM call ([arXiv 2603.02830](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02830)).

| Model | What it gives | Data needed | Verdict for Sapling |
|---|---|---|---|
| BKT (4 params, per-channel G/S, forgetting) | Calibrated P(known) per concept; likelihood-ratio semantics for evidence weight | None to start (literature priors); 50 students × 15 opportunities to refit | **Primary.** Exactly what MATHia does; the only model where "how much should one chat turn move mastery" is derived, not tuned |
| Elo / PFAE with uncertainty schedule | Logit-scale skill + item difficulty; one line per update; never degenerates | None | **Secondary**, kept only if item difficulty is needed for question selection; loses the P(known) semantics |
| IRT 1PL/2PL/3PL | Ability + item parameters; assumes constant skill | Regularized MLE on hundreds of items | Not for a learning model ("applicable only for short tests where we do not expect learning") ([Pelánek 2016](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf)) |
| DKT / SAKT / AKT | Recency, inter-skill similarity, individual ability | Thousands of learners; chance-level at n≈150 dialogues | Not viable at this scale |
| Knowledge Space Theory (ALEKS) | Distribution over closed knowledge states; fringes | Enumerable state space; ALEKS partitions 10^23 states into substructures | Borrow the **fringe logic** for planning and the **assessment rules** for probing; do not build the state space |
| Dynamic Bayesian net over prerequisites | +0.09 to +0.20 AUC on structured domains | 2^(n−1) CPT parameters per node, loopy inference | Borrow a one-hop noisy-AND propagation; skip full inference |

The update equations, as formalized by van de Sande, are three closed-form lines per answer. With P(L) the current belief, P(T) the learning rate, P(G) the guess rate and P(S) the slip rate ([van de Sande 2013](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1115329.pdf)):

```
after CORRECT:   P(L | obs) = P(L)·(1−S) / [ P(L)·(1−S) + (1−P(L))·G ]
after INCORRECT: P(L | obs) = P(L)·S     / [ P(L)·S     + (1−P(L))·(1−G) ]
then learn:      P(L') = P(L | obs) + (1 − P(L | obs))·T
```

Validity requires **G + S < 1 and 0 < T < 1 − S/(1−G)**; if G + S exceeds 1 the update inverts and correct answers lower belief ([van de Sande 2013](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1115329.pdf)). Cognitive Tutors bound **G ≤ 0.3 and S ≤ 0.1** and call any fit with G or S above 0.5 "theoretically degenerate"; unbounded EM produced fits where three correct answers in a row lowered mastery for 23% of skills ([Baker, Corbett & Aleven 2008](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/BCA2008W.pdf)). A 2024 first-principles derivation adds the steady-state condition P(L0) > (1−G)·T/(1−S−G) and shows constrained fitting produced zero degenerate parameter sets where Baum-Welch produced 20% ([EDM 2024](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-long-papers.2/index.html)). Sapling should start with fixed priors in the range the literature uses — P(L0) ≈ 0.3–0.4, T ≈ 0.1–0.2 (van de Sande's worked example uses L0 = 0.36, T = 0.1, G = 0.3, S = 0.05) — and refit nightly with pyBKT (`multigs=True, forgets=True`) only for concepts that clear the 50-student bar, accepting fitted values only inside the constraints ([pyBKT](https://github.com/CAHLR/pyBKT)). Seeding P(L0) per concept from the probe rather than a global constant mirrors ALEKS, whose prior over states "is informed by past assessment data from the course" ([Matayoshi & Cosyn 2021](https://jmatayoshi.github.io/publications/JMP2021_KST_ALEKS_preprint.pdf)).

Evidence weighting is where BKT earns its place over a hand-tuned delta. The model-native mechanism is a separate (G, S) pair per evidence channel — the KT-IDEM idea, which adds ≈ 0.02 AUC per item class ([Badrinath et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00385)) and whose contextual form raised A′ from 0.66 to 0.75 in the original study ([Baker et al. 2008](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/BCA2008W.pdf)). ALEKS is the worked example: open-response items make a lucky guess "very small" so a correct answer moves the state substantially, an incorrect answer "cannot be very aggressive given that there is a non-negligible chance for a careless error," and an explicit "I don't know" button lets the system update aggressively downward ([Matayoshi & Cosyn 2021](https://jmatayoshi.github.io/publications/JMP2021_KST_ALEKS_preprint.pdf)). For multiple choice with k options, Pelánek's shifted logistic floors success at 1/k, and IRT's pseudo-guessing c is typically 0.15–0.25 for 4–5 options ([Pelánek 2016](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf); [Iowa RRC](https://irrc.education.uiowa.edu/blog/2020/09/technically-speaking-determining-test-effectiveness-item-response-theory)). For LLM-judged chat turns the one direct measurement is GPT-4o labelling Khanmigo dialogue turns at **75.8–83.3% final-turn correctness accuracy versus 85.6% for human annotators**, and KT models trained on those labels reach only AUC 0.53–0.66 versus > 0.80 on item data ([Scarlatos, Baker & Lan 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16490)). The FCI designers put the false-negative rate for a competent student below 10% ([Hestenes & Halloun 1995](https://davidhestenes.net/modeling/R&E/InterFCI.pdf)), which is where S ≈ 0.10 comes from. The table below is a defensible starting prior; the values are inferred from these accuracy numbers, not fitted, and are what the nightly refit should replace.

| Evidence channel | G (guess) | S (slip) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free response graded against a reference answer / rubric | 0.05–0.10 | 0.10 | ALEKS open-response; FCI < 10% false negatives |
| 4-option multiple choice, no reason | 0.25–0.30 | 0.10 | 1/k floor; Cognitive Tutor G ≤ 0.3 bound |
| Multiple choice + correct one-sentence reason (tier 2) | 0.10 | 0.10 | Right-answer-wrong-reason removed as a false positive [inference] |
| Teach-back / self-explanation graded by LLM rubric | 0.25 | 0.20 | 76–83% label accuracy on dialogue turns |
| Tutor-chat turn judged by the model | 0.30 | 0.30 | Weak observation; G + S → 1 contributes nothing, which is the correct limit for an unreliable judge |
| Explicit "I don't know" | — | 0.02 | ALEKS: update aggressively downward |
| Same-session re-check after feedback | halve the observation | | Unvalidated heuristic; no study measured this weight. Motivation only: the testing effect reverses at 5 min (restudy 81% vs test 75%) and appears at 2 days and 1 week, with no feedback on the tests ([Roediger & Karpicke 2006](https://colinallen.dnsalias.org/Readings/2006_Roediger_Karpicke_PsychSci.pdf)) |

A worked example shows why this matters more than the exact numbers. From P(L) = 0.30 with T = 0.15, one correct free response (G = 0.08, S = 0.10) lifts belief to 0.83 before learning and 0.85 after; one correct 4-option MC (G = 0.25) lifts it only to 0.61 then 0.67; one "correct" chat turn (G = S = 0.30) lifts it to 0.50. From P(L) = 0.90, one wrong free response drops belief to 0.49 before learning and 0.57 after — which is exactly the "re-ask an isomorphic item" trigger in the slip rule below — and a second wrong, from 0.57, drops it to 0.13 before learning and 0.26 after. Today's tutor can move the score by +0.3 on a turn in which nothing was graded; under this model the same turn moves it by at most the chat-channel likelihood ratio, and a graded check dominates it. If the grader returns partial credit, mix the two emission rows by the score rather than thresholding at 0.5 (Wang & Heffernan's continuous-observation extension, cited in [Van Inwegen et al. 2015](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED560588.pdf)); when a check touches several concepts, update only the ones the grader names, with the observation weight split evenly (the DAS3H multi-KC framing, [Choffin et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.06873)).

Forgetting belongs in the same row. Without it, "once BKT infers that the student has learned, no subsequent evidence can alter the inferred knowledge state"; adding a forget transition per intervening trial lifted ASSISTments AUC from 0.73 to 0.83 ([Khajah et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.02416)). Pelánek's hypothesis is that forgetting matters greatly for facts and fluency and only slightly for understanding ([Pelánek 2017](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/umuai-overview.pdf)), which argues for making decay a per-concept quantity owned by the scheduler. The cleanest composition — a synthesis, not a cited design — is to store P(L) and `last_evidence_at`, and at read time decay toward the prior with the scheduler's retrievability: `P(L)_now = L0 + (P(L) − L0)·R(Δ, S_c)`, where R is the FSRS-6 curve and S_c the concept's stability (next section), then run the Bayes step. This is BKT+F with a time-varying F and keeps every write O(1).

Prerequisite propagation should be one hop, damped, and asymmetric. Knowledge Space Theory's closure property means every state containing an advanced item contains its prerequisites, so a correct answer high in the graph raises all states that include the prerequisites ([Matayoshi & Cosyn 2021](https://jmatayoshi.github.io/publications/JMP2021_KST_ALEKS_preprint.pdf)); Knewton's "proficiency propagation" infers prerequisite mastery from success on dependents without direct evidence ([Knewton alta](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/grow/teach-learn/teacher-resources/courseware/knewton-alta/resources/alta-blog-how-does-knewton-proficiency-model-estimate/)); Pelánek's multivariate Elo does the same with a correlation-damped update θ_j += c_ij·K·(correct − P) ([Pelánek 2016](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf)). Full DBN inference with prerequisite CPTs buys +0.09 to +0.20 AUC on structured domains but costs 2^(n−1) parameters per node ([Käser et al. 2017](https://cgl.ethz.ch/Downloads/Publications/Papers/2017/Kae17a/Kae17a.pdf)), which Sapling neither needs nor can fit. The rule that follows: on a correct answer at concept c, apply the Bayes step to each parent with a heavily attenuated observation (treat it as a chat-strength observation); on an incorrect answer at c, leave parents alone — an error can be local — and apply the attenuated downward step to c's children, since in the closure any state containing a child contains c. Keep BKT parameters keyed on the abstract course concept, not the offering, so cross-offering pooling can reach the 50-student refit bar.

Thresholds: MATHia declares mastery at P(L) > 0.95 per knowledge component ([Ritter et al. 2016](https://www.educationaldatamining.org/EDM2016/proceedings/paper_187.pdf)), but the 2025 Rori study binned end-of-lesson beliefs and found students at **≥ 0.98 had significantly higher next-lesson accuracy than those at 0.95–0.98 (estimate 0.041, p < 0.001)** ([Zhang, Vanacore, Baker et al. 2025](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED675652.pdf)). ALEKS requires a target score of 5 (two correct in a row on an open item) before calling an item learned, and its progress assessments can remove items later ([Matayoshi & Cosyn 2021](https://jmatayoshi.github.io/publications/JMP2021_KST_ALEKS_preprint.pdf)). So: **proficient = P ≥ 0.95; mastered = P ≥ 0.98 AND ≥ 3 observations on strong channels (free response or MC-plus-reason)**, and both labels can fall when a review is missed. Sapling's 0.1/0.45/0.75 tiers have no analogue in any deployed system and should go. Schema-wise this is `concept_params(concept_id, channel, L0, T, G, S)`, `learner_state(user_id, concept_id, p_known, n_obs_strong, last_evidence_at)`, and an append-only `evidence(user_id, concept_id, channel, correct, weight, ts)` log that can be replayed after a refit — the same reason `node_mastery_events` is already append-only.

## Run each session as probe, plan, step, check, feedback, close

VanLehn's minimum tutor has an outer loop that picks the next task and updates the student model, and an inner loop that gives feedback and hints on each step; the inner loop is what makes a system an ITS at all ([VanLehn 2006](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/IRG-2006-16%283%2902)). Sapling's tutor today has neither: three prompt modes, no phases, and the model's own judgment as the student model. The rules below fill both loops.

### Probe: 4–6 adaptive items, aimed at 50–70% success, with a floor rule for novices

Computerized adaptive testing supplies the numbers. Fisher information is maximal when the student has a 50% chance on the item; targeting 70% costs about 20% more items and 90% nearly triples test length; standard error is 1/√ΣP(1−P) and shrinks slowly ([Linacre, Rasch Measurement Transactions](https://www.rasch.org/rmt/rmt202f.htm)). With a stopping rule of SE ≤ 0.3 logits, simulated CATs converge in **4–6 items** from a 30-item pool; PROMIS defaults to a minimum of 4, a maximum of 12, or SE < 0.32 ([Choi, Grady & Dodd 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3028267/); [PROMIS stopping rules](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12681468/)). The critical failure mode is the total novice: at θ ≈ −2 a fixed-SE rule "would administer all 28 items in the pool, even though the desired standard error is unobtainable," because information is scarce at extreme ability ([Choi et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3028267/)). ALEKS caps its initial assessment at 30 questions, picks each item so that the states containing it have ≈ 0.5 probability, calls an item in-state above 80% likelihood and out-of-state below 20%, and fast-tracks "uncertain" items in learning mode ([Matayoshi & Cosyn 2021](https://jmatayoshi.github.io/publications/JMP2021_KST_ALEKS_preprint.pdf)). Its held-out validity is AUROC 0.86–0.89 on millions of assessments — the strongest evidence anywhere that a prerequisite-structured adaptive probe measures something real.

Sapling's probe rule: start at the outer fringe of the last known state (or at the syllabus midpoint for a new course), pick the next item at 50–70% expected success under the current belief, ask 4–6 items per skill with a hard session cap of 8–12, and stop when belief has stabilized. Use multiple choice with an explicit "I don't know" option for speed, but require a one-sentence reason before a correct MC answer is credited at strong-channel weight — two-tier tests exist because "the correct answers to multiple choice items do not guarantee the right reason being present," and three-tier tests add confidence to separate misconception from ignorance ([four-tier review](https://www.tused.org/index.php/tused/article/download/1180/819/7915); [Hestenes & Halloun 1995](https://davidhestenes.net/modeling/R&E/InterFCI.pdf)). For the novice: **three consecutive misses at the easiest available level ends the probe**, classifies the student as below the item bank, and switches to teaching the lowest unmastered prerequisite from the inner fringe. Because LLM-generated items have no calibrated difficulty parameters, "maximum information" here is a heuristic, not a psychometric guarantee — this is the one part of the probe that is an extrapolation.

### Plan: teach at the outer fringe, over prerequisites, three to five new concepts at most

The zone of proximal development operationalized is KST's outer fringe: items not in the state whose addition still yields a valid state, "the set of items the student is ready to learn" ([ALEKS KST](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks/knowledge_space_theory)). Building on prerequisites is one of the strategies Graesser's analysis of 100+ hours of naturalistic tutoring found human tutors rarely use ([Graesser et al., AutoTutor chapter](https://blogs.memphis.edu/aolney/files/2019/10/AutoTutor-chapter-olney_publications.pdf)); an AI can do it mechanically, and Khan Academy's vendor A/B found surfacing prerequisite gaps was worth +2.7% next-item correctness ([Khan blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)). The planner should therefore replace "five lowest-mastery nodes" with: the set of concepts whose prerequisites are all proficient, filtered by the course goal (syllabus week, upcoming assignment), ordered by due reviews first. Cognitive load theory's operative limit is not concepts per hour but two to three novel interacting elements in working memory at once ([Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5)); the classroom protocols that worked used 8–10 definitions per set and ~7.6 study-to-proficiency trials per item ([UNH successive-relearning guide](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-successive-relearning-dunlosky-greve-badali-wissman-rawson.pdf); [Lindsey et al. 2014](https://library.scottbarrykaufman.com/uploads/2014/01/Lindsey-et-al.-2014.pdf)). Cap a session at 3–5 new concepts when they are independent and 1–2 when tightly coupled, order it as stable reviews → new material → interleaved siblings, and cap by minutes rather than count, letting due reviews overflow to tomorrow. Students should approve the path (autonomy over *what*, below), but not the step size or hint depth.

### Teach one step: state-dependent assistance, ≤ 3–5 sentences, student moves next

The step is where expertise reversal and productive failure decide the format. For a novice on a concept, high assistance wins (d = 0.505); for a student with prior knowledge it hurts (d = −0.428), and the asymmetry means "when uncertain, provide support" ([Tetzlaff et al. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475225000660)). For secondary-age and older learners with a conceptual goal, a bounded struggle attempt *before* instruction adds g = 0.36 on understanding and transfer, up to 0.58 with high design fidelity ([Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105)). The two are compatible as a sequence: a novice step is (1) one bounded attempt of 1–3 minutes at a target problem with no answer visible, (2) a worked example or direct explanation in small chunks, (3) a near-transfer problem with step-level hints. A developing student skips (2) and goes problem-first with hints on request; a proficient student gets the problem and verification-only feedback (Shute's guidelines 26–29, [Shute 2007](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202007_f.pdf)). Fading is a per-concept state driven by the last outcome — Wood's contingent shift: more support after failure, less after success ([Van de Pol, Volman & Beishuizen 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-010-9127-6)) — not by elapsed time.

Turn shape follows the multimedia meta-analyses. Learner-paced segmenting beats system-paced on transfer, d = 0.45 versus 0.35 ([Rey et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4)); signaling the one key element gives g = 0.52 on retention ([Schneider et al. 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X17300581)); seductive details cost retention and transfer ([Rey 2012](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ986386)); Kestin's tutor was told to answer in "no more than a few sentences" to avoid overload ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)). So each turn is one idea, ≤ 3–5 sentences, one named key element, no asides, ending with an explicit action for the student — never auto-advancing. Chi's finding that students learned just as well when tutors were restricted to prompting ([Chi et al. 2001](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/learning-from-human-tutoring/)) and the ICAP ordering interactive > constructive > active > passive ([Chi & Wylie 2014](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chiwylie2014icap_2.pdf)) both say the learning comes from what the student constructs, so an LLM's fluency is not where the leverage is. VanLehn's granularity result says scaffold at the solution-step level, not below it.

### Check: free response and teach-back for credit, multiple choice for speed

Retrieval of any format beats restudy, but the formats differ in what they measure and what they cause. With corrective feedback, short-answer practice beat multiple choice even on a multiple-choice final (d = 0.41) ([Kang, McDermott & Roediger 2007](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zTxLkeaWCdBHQYW73o5n6Ka)); harder successful retrieval produces more learning ([Pyc & Rawson 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0749596X09000138)); retrieval beat concept mapping on inference questions a week later ([Karpicke & Blunt 2011](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1199327)). The two meta-analyses disagree on format — Rowland's lab-only synthesis favors recall, Adesope's classroom-inclusive one favors multiple choice and a format-match bonus ([Frontiers 2018 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02412/full)) — so "free response always" is not safe; match some practice to the course's exam format. Self-explanation prompts (g = 0.55) and teach-back (g = 0.56 when the student actually teaches) are both checks and interventions ([Bisra et al. 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x); [Kobayashi 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jpr.12221)). Never accept "I get it": active-learning students scored 0.46 SD higher and rated their learning 0.56 SD lower than lecture peers ([Deslauriers et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6765278/)), and judgments of learning are inflated whenever the target is in view at study ([Koriat & Bjork 2005](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/07/Koriat_RBjork_2005.pdf)). Concept-inventory validity "arises primarily from its distractors," which are harvested from real student reasoning ([AAAS Richardson](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/02_AER_Richardson.pdf)); Sapling should harvest actual wrong reasons over time rather than trusting model-invented distractors.

### Feedback: high-information, after an attempt, never ending in the answer

Content dominates timing. Task + process + self-regulation feedback gives d = 0.99; corrective alone 0.46; praise and reinforcement 0.24; immediate versus delayed is "inconsistent" ([Wisniewski et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987456/)). Shute's guidelines translate directly into tutor policy: feedback only after an attempt, in manageable stepwise units, task-focused and never person-focused, no normative comparison, comments rather than grades mid-loop, praise "sparingly, if at all," no interruption of a student mid-solution, no exhaustive error diagnosis, and — guideline 16 — "avoid using progressive hints that always terminate with the correct answer" ([Shute 2007](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202007_f.pdf)). The consistent timing moderator is learner level: immediate, directive, elaborated feedback for novices and hard tasks; delayed, facilitative prompts for proficient students and transfer goals. AutoTutor's expectation-misconception tailoring — compare the answer against a small set of expected good answers and anticipated misconceptions, then pump/hint/prompt/assert — is the evidence-backed depth of diagnosis ([Graesser AutoTutor chapter](https://blogs.memphis.edu/aolney/files/2019/10/AutoTutor-chapter-olney_publications.pdf)); constraint-based modeling shows the same can be done by encoding only correct-domain constraints rather than a bug library ([Mitrović & Ohlsson 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-015-0075-7)). In Sapling terms: the concept map carries a short "expected answers + common wrong reasons" block per concept, and the tutor matches against it.

### Slip, misconception, or novice: three different responses

The BKT slip parameter is a performance statistic, not a diagnosis, and a single wrong answer is uninformative. The FCI designers' rule is to probe each dimension with several questions in different contexts so one false positive is offset, and never to interpret single items ([Hestenes & Halloun 1995](https://davidhestenes.net/modeling/R&E/InterFCI.pdf)); the tiered-test literature adds confidence as the discriminator between misconception and ignorance. The decision rule: **one wrong answer = unknown**, move belief by the channel's likelihood ratio and re-ask an isomorphic item in a different surface context; **wrong then right on the isomorph = slip**, restore belief and continue; **wrong on ≥ 2 isomorphs with the same distractor family, or wrong with high stated confidence = misconception**, confront it (Lehman's trialogue work found learning gains only when a contradiction actually induced confusion and the student resolved it, [Lehman et al. 2013](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1190004)) and record it on the learner model; **wrong with low confidence = lack of knowledge**, teach; **correct answer with wrong reasoning = not known**, treat as a miss. **A novice** — three misses at the floor, or "I don't know" repeatedly — exits probing and gets the novice step format on the lowest prerequisite; the CAT literature is explicit that more probing there only burns items.

### Difficulty band: hold running step-level success near 75–90%

The 85% rule is a theorem about stochastic-gradient learners on binary classification: optimal training error is 15.87%, and the optimum shifts with noise distribution and is explicitly "to be generalized to multi-choice tasks and different learning algorithms" ([Wilson et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12552-4)). It is model-only, but it agrees with psychophysics staircases and desirable-difficulty practice, so use it as a control target rather than a claim: keep running practice accuracy within ≈ 75–90%; step up above 90% (boredom risk), step down or scaffold below ≈ 70% with rising latency (frustration risk). Productive failure is a sequence decision for new concepts; the band is a steady-state target after instruction.

### Close: a written summary, a self-evaluation, an if-then plan

Sapling's session summaries are scraped, never read back; the remodel's arrows 11 and 18 fix this cheaply. Three things belong in the close. First, a model-written summary of what was checked, what moved, and open misconceptions, stored on the learner model and injected next session as a short learner brief — Khan's vendor A/B found recent-history summaries worth +3.4% ([Khan blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)). Second, a self-evaluation prompt ("which step were you least sure of?"): Fan et al.'s 117-student RCT found ChatGPT users' self-regulation "centred on ChatGPT" and lacked the orientation↔evaluation transitions human tutors trigger, with better essays but no knowledge-gain or transfer difference ([Fan et al. 2025](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.13544)). Third, an implementation intention for the next session: if-then plans raise goal attainment d = 0.65 across 94 tests ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement:-A-Gollwitzer-Sheeran/c4deb3507fe725ce6363c1735f1ba83bab20d665)), and MCII raised PSAT practice completion from 84 to 140 questions ([Duckworth et al. 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ911106)). Then write the evidence rows, update the scheduler, and stop.

## Schedule review with FSRS-6 at 90% retention and successive relearning

Sapling has no decay, no forgetting curve and no review schedule; the closest thing is the quiz's do-not-repeat list. The magnitude of what this leaves on the table is large. Testing beats restudy by 14 points at one week (56% vs 42%, d = 0.83) while losing at five minutes ([Roediger & Karpicke 2006](https://colinallen.dnsalias.org/Readings/2006_Roediger_Karpicke_PsychSci.pdf)); spacing beats massing 47.3% to 36.7% across 254 comparisons, with the gap widening at longer delays (32.8 → 62.2 at 8–30 days) ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://augmentingcognition.com/assets/Cepeda2006.pdf)); the optimal gap is roughly 10–20% of the retention interval for delays of weeks and 5–10% for a year ([Cepeda et al. 2008](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x)). Dunlosky's review rates practice testing and distributed practice as the only two high-utility techniques of ten ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1529100612453266)). And the classroom result that matters most for a semester course: Lindsey et al. randomized each chapter's items *within student* to massed, generic-spaced, and personalized (DASH model + closest-to-threshold) review; at ~30 minutes a week of review, **personalized beat massed by 16.5 points (d = 1.42) and generic spacing by 10.0 points (d = 0.88) on a 28-day-delayed cumulative exam**, with the benefit concentrated on early-semester chapters ([Lindsey et al. 2014](https://library.scottbarrykaufman.com/uploads/2014/01/Lindsey-et-al.-2014.pdf)).

FSRS-6 is the memory model to adopt, with its defaults as priors. Each concept carries Difficulty D ∈ [1,10], Stability S in days (time for retrievability to fall to 0.9), and Retrievability R ([ABC of FSRS](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS)). The equations ([The Algorithm](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/The-Algorithm)):

```
R(t,S)        = (1 + factor·t/S)^(−w20),   factor = 0.9^(−1/w20) − 1
I(r,S)        = (S/factor)·(r^(−1/w20) − 1)          # interval for desired retention r
S0(G)         = w[G−1]                                  # first rating G∈{1 Again,2 Hard,3 Good,4 Easy}
D0(G)         = w4 − e^(w5·(G−1)) + 1,  clamp [1,10]
ΔD            = −w6·(G−3);  D' = D + ΔD·(10−D)/9;  D'' = w7·D0(4) + (1−w7)·D'
S'_recall     = S·( e^(w8)·(11−D)·S^(−w9)·(e^(w10·(1−R)) − 1)·[w15 if Hard]·[w16 if Easy] + 1 )
S'_lapse      = w11·D^(−w12)·((S+1)^(w13) − 1)·e^(w14·(1−R))
S'_same_day   = S·e^(w17·(G−3+w18))·S^(−w19)
w (FSRS-6)    = [0.212, 1.2931, 2.3065, 8.2956, 6.4133, 0.8334, 3.0194, 0.001, 1.8722, 0.1666,
                 0.796, 1.4835, 0.0614, 0.2629, 1.6483, 0.6014, 1.8729, 0.5425, 0.0912, 0.0658, 0.1542]
```

The (e^(w10·(1−R)) − 1) term is the spacing effect built in: a successful recall at R = 0.7 grows stability more than one at R = 0.95. The permitted retention range is 0.70–0.97, "90% should work fine for most people," and above 0.97 the schedule collapses into massed practice ([fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md)); the optimizer minimizes workload/knowledge by simulation, typically landing near 0.85 ([optimal retention](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/wiki/The-optimal-retention)). On 500M+ reviews FSRS-6 beats SM-2 on log-loss for 99.6% of users with 20–30% fewer reviews at equal retention ([srs-benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark); [ABC of FSRS](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS)). SM-2 (I(1)=1, I(2)=6, I(n)=I(n−1)·EF, EF from 2.5 with floor 1.3) has no notion of retrievability and cannot answer "what has this student most likely forgotten right now" ([SM-2](https://www.super-memory.com/english/ol/sm2.htm)), so it should not be the production scheduler. Duolingo's half-life regression, `p = 2^(−Δ/h)`, `ĥ = 2^(Θ·x)` over square-rooted correct/incorrect counts, is the easiest form for heterogeneous evidence and gave +9.5% practice retention and +12% activity in production — but its AUC was 0.538, barely above chance, so its win is calibration, not ranking ([Settles & Meeder 2016](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf)).

Targets and rules for Sapling: desired retention **r = 0.90 by default, 0.85 for large concept sets when a CMRR-style simulation says so, 0.95 only in the 1–2 weeks before an exam**. Map each check to a rating with a format-aware rule — free response or teach-back correct and unprompted → Good (Easy only if fluent), MC correct → Good with a capped stability multiplier, correct after a hint or partially correct → Hard, wrong → Again — and store check type and pre-check R on every review row so w0–w3 and w8–w10 can be refit once a few thousand concept reviews exist; no published scheduler maps MCQ versus teach-back onto stability, so this mapping is engineered and must be validated. When the due list exceeds the daily budget, order by |R − 0.33|: the DASH threshold policy at T = 0.33 was "only slightly" worse than the intractable optimum under a fixed budget ([Lindsey et al. 2014](https://library.scottbarrykaufman.com/uploads/2014/01/Lindsey-et-al.-2014.pdf)). Wrap it in the successive-relearning state machine, the closest published protocol to a college-course review loop: retrieve with feedback to **3 correct recalls, then relearn to 1 correct recall in 3 spaced sessions** ([Rawson & Dunlosky 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ934616)); in an intro psychology course this raised exam scores > 10 points on application questions and 24-day recall from < 20% to > 60%, with sessions falling from 30–40 minutes to under 5 by the third relearn ([UNH guide](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-successive-relearning-dunlosky-greve-badali-wissman-rawson.pdf)), and students reported less anxiety and better metacognition ([Rawson & Dunlosky 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09637214221100484)). A daily budget of 10–15 minutes (≈ 10–20 concept checks) is consistent with the 30 min/week that produced d ≈ 1.4; there is no primary source for a maximum sustainable reviews/day. Cepeda's ratio rule is the sanity bound: for a final 12 weeks out, first gaps of 1–2.5 weeks, not one day; FSRS at r = 0.9 will start shorter, which is fine for first consolidation. Same-session re-checks are not durable evidence (FSRS-6 has a separate same-day update for the same reason).

Interleaving belongs in the review pool, not in first acquisition. Interleaved practice on four solid-volume formulas gave 63% versus 20% blocked a week later (d = 1.34) despite worse practice-phase performance ([Rohrer & Taylor 2007](http://uweb.cas.usf.edu/~drohrer/pdfs/Rohrer&Taylor2007IS.pdf)), replicated in a 7th-grade cluster RCT ([Rohrer, Dedrick & Stershic 2015](http://uweb.cas.usf.edu/~drohrer/pdfs/Rohrer_et_al_2015JEdPsych.pdf)); the meta-analytic g = 0.42 is driven by confusable categories and is weak or absent for expository text ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)). So mix due reviews of *sibling* concepts (same parent in the graph) and require the student to say which concept applies before applying it; mixing unrelated chapters buys only the spacing benefit plus switch cost. Flashcards take facts and vocabulary; the review quiz takes concepts; both go through the same grader so reviews count as evidence (remodel arrows 22–24).

## Hold the difficulty band and treat affect as a state machine, not a mood ring

The validated affect model from ITS research is a state machine: engaged/flow → impasse → confusion → either resolved (back to flow, with learning) or unresolved → frustration → boredom ([D'Mello & Graesser 2012](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ950444)). Boredom is the state to fear — it is the one linked to gaming the system and poor outcomes, hence "better to be frustrated than bored" ([Baker, D'Mello, Rodrigo & Graesser 2010, summary](https://www.globallearningcouncil.org/posts/gaming-the-system-affect-detectors-and-student-persistence-and-learning-in-computer-based-learning-environments/); [Baker, IJAIED](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/STS-Baker-IJAIED-v15.pdf)). Confusion helps only when a real impasse induces it and the student resolves it ([Lehman et al. 2013](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1190004)). An affect-sensitive AutoTutor improved learning for low-knowledge students, at deeper comprehension, and only in the second session, while being less effective for high-knowledge students early ([D'Mello, Lehman & Graesser 2011](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4419-9625-1_9)). Baker's log-based gaming detectors key on fast action after errors, help abuse, and systematic guessing ([Baker IJAIED](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/STS-Baker-IJAIED-v15.pdf)). No peer-reviewed validation of chat-app proxies (hedging words, typing latency) for LLM tutors exists, so the mapping is an inference: sub-second resubmit after an error or a chain of "just tell me" without an attempt → gaming/help abuse; long latency plus a hedged or question-form reply → productive confusion, which the tutor should let the student resolve; rising error count on one subgoal with rising negativity → frustration, answered with support plus a smaller next step; flat minimal replies or abandonment → boredom, answered with a shakeup (harder task, format change, a deliberate contradiction). Gate supportive affect responses on estimated mastery; they help novices and can hurt experts.

Gamification evidence is small and conditional. Sailer & Homner's meta-analysis gives g = 0.49 for cognitive outcomes (stable under rigorous designs), 0.36 motivational and 0.25 behavioral (not stable) ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270)). The most ecologically valid college result is negative: a 16-week course section with leaderboard and badges showed lower intrinsic motivation, satisfaction and final-exam scores, mediated by motivation ([Hanus & Fox 2015](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Assessing-the-effects-of-gamification-in-the-A-on-Hanus-Fox/dff76a9862467d426113ec530f83942016ae3a97)), and Deci's 128-experiment meta-analysis found tangible contingent rewards undermine intrinsic motivation while informational feedback does not ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-meta-analytic-review-of-experiments-examining-the-Deci-Koestner/8ad9801baea65b40fbbe6fc56e34b2b7be47d0ba)). Streaks work through loss aversion and the damage is at the break: a broken streak reduces subsequent engagement, more when self-attributed, less when repairable ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/49/6/1095/6623414)); Duolingo's own A/B found a weekend amulet made users 4% more likely to return and that bingers churned more ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals/), vendor). Rules: levels tied to mastery (a competence signal), no leaderboards or ranked badges in a course context, weekly rather than strict daily streaks with a freeze, no credit for binge sessions. Growth-mindset messaging is cheap and small — ~0.05–0.10 SD overall, ~0.16 for at-risk students, with a prediction interval crossing zero ([Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023](https://artscimedia.case.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/141/2020/06/26110416/Macnamara-Burgoyne-2023.pdf); [Tipton et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10495100/)) — and the one robust moderator is norm alignment, so embed error-as-information framing in feedback rather than shipping a mindset module. Learning styles are out: the meshing hypothesis fails the crossover test, with only 26% of outcomes showing the required pattern and a warning about "psychological essentialism" ([Clinton-Lisell & Litzinger 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11270031/)); pick representation by content (spatial → integrated diagram) and by mastery, never by a self-reported style.

Autonomy should be given over goals, topic order and pacing, and withheld over difficulty and hint depth. Choice raises intrinsic motivation and effort ([Patall, Cooper & Robinson 2008](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ787695)), and intrinsic and identified motivation predict persistence across 344 samples, though with small correlations to objective outcomes (ρ ≈ 0.11–0.13) ([Howard et al. 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691620966789)); but learner control over instructional sequencing in ed-tech has g = 0.05 ([Karich, Burns & Maki 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654314526064)), and students systematically prefer the format they learn less from ([Deslauriers et al. 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.1821936116)). Peers supply what a 1:1 AI cannot. Peer Instruction doubled Mazur's normalized gain from 0.25 to 0.49 in the first semester and 0.74 by the sixth ([Crouch & Mazur 2001](https://pubs.aip.org/aapt/ajp/article/69/9/970/310529/Peer-Instruction-Ten-years-of-experience-and)), and peer discussion improved performance on isomorphic questions even when nobody in the group initially knew the answer ([Smith et al. 2009](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1165919)); pedagogical-agent "social presence" yields only small gains, smaller for post-secondary students ([Schroeder et al. 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2190/EC.49.1.a)). Sapling's rooms should implement the PI mechanism — commit alone to the AI, see the distribution, discuss, re-vote, explanation — and use aggregate progress and shared misconceptions (above the n ≥ 5 floor) as the social signal, never a ranking.

## Guardrails: withhold answers, ground in verified solutions, gate the grader

Neither study isolates a single design difference: Bastani's no-harm arm bundled hint-not-answer prompting with teacher-provided solutions and common-mistake guidance, and Kestin compared its tutor with an active-learning class rather than with a no-harm tutor, so reading scaffolding against instructor-authored solutions as what separates no harm from a large gain is a cross-study hypothesis. Three mechanisms explain the harm. Students copy: GPT Base conversations were dominated by asking for and copying solutions, and students "were not aware of how generative AI can impede their learning" ([Bastani et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)). Models err: GPT-4 gave correct answers on only **51% of the curriculum practice problems** (42% logical, 8% arithmetic errors), which is why the safe arm bundled teacher solutions — hint-not-answer is only safe when the tutor has a verified answer to hint toward. Models cave: on the EduFrameTrap benchmark of 3,240 dialogues, frontier models retreated from a correct correction under student pressure **14.0–14.2% of the time**, with a "reasoning–sycophancy paradox" in which stronger reasoning did not buy resilience ([arXiv 2605.14604](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14604v1)). The harm also appears when students bring an outside chatbot to a mature ITS: on a 3.2M-interaction ALEKS panel, post-ChatGPT learning time on AI-susceptible problems fell 26.9% for college students over 11 quarters and odds of a correct **proctored** retention item fell 25%, while unproctored scores rose ([Rismanchian et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21629), working paper). LearnLM's authors concluded "most pedagogy is too nuanced to be explained with prompting" and that prompting produced unreliable pedagogy, motivating fine-tuning ([Jurenka et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12687v3)); MathTutorBench found simple questioning strategies degrade in longer dialogues ([arXiv 2502.18940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18940)).

The recurring stack across the deployments that worked is: verified answer key in context → deterministic sequencer outside the model → hint-not-answer instruction → small judge model on risky turns → curriculum or human wrapper. The most concrete published architecture computes a per-turn help ceiling from **trusted learner state only, never from student text** (so prompt injection cannot raise it), runs an eight-rung ladder from H0 "acknowledge and encourage" to H7 "show full solution," strips solutions with a deterministic detector before any LLM judge, uses a small temperature-0 judge only on risky turns, and passed gates of zero solution reveals, ≤ 5% revision rate on earnest help-seekers and ≥ 95% ceiling compliance under adversarial personas — with an explicit "no learning-outcome claim" ([arXiv 2608.12292](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12292)). Its observed failure ladder — partial code under pressure, ceilings too low for debugging, fabricated citations — is the test list Sapling should adopt. Reconciling Shute's "no hint chain that ends in the answer" with H7: the full solution is reachable only after two genuine attempts, only for practice items the learner model marks as taught, and never for items tagged as graded coursework. The policy layer must be Sapling code, not the prompt; today the tutor's three modes and hard-coded prompt have no such layer.

Grading reliability sets the ceiling on free-text and teach-back evidence. For short, rubric-anchored STEM answers GPT-4-class graders reach human level: **70–80% agreement on a three-item binary physics rubric, equal to or above human-human agreement** ([Phys. Rev. PER 2025](https://journals.aps.org/prper/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.21.010126)); κ = 0.75 on K-12 short answers ([arXiv 2405.02985](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.02985)); QWK 0.677 across ten questions ([Jiang, L@S 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3657604.3664685)); Pearson 0.98 on quiz totals but only 55% exact match, with **GPT-4o under-grading 38.8% and over-grading 6.2%** and divergence on stylistic criteria ([LLM-as-a-Grader 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10819v1)). The rubric format that works is itemized, binary or few-level criteria each tied to a reference answer, scored per item; holistic style scores fail. Design for κ ≈ 0.7 as the realistic ceiling: the grader always sees the reference answer (never solves the problem itself — the 51% number), returns per-item binary judgments plus confidence, low-confidence grades are weighted down or routed to a second model, and no single LLM grade is both mastery-deciding and unrevisable. Strictness is the safer bias (it triggers more practice); leniency plus sycophancy is the dangerous pair. Group fairness of LLM grading is an open question the notes could not quantify.

## Measure learning with tool-removed, delayed checks, not in-session accuracy

Learning and performance are different quantities, and Sapling currently measures only the wrong one. Soderstrom and Bjork's review is the frame: current performance is an unreliable guide to learning, which is inferred only from delayed retention or transfer, and desirable difficulties depress the former while raising the latter ([Soderstrom & Bjork 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1745691615569000)). Bastani's harm was invisible in practice data and visible only on the unassisted exam; the ALEKS panel's proctored and unproctored items moved in opposite directions. Khan Academy's production metric — next-item correctness after a thread, A/B at 95% confidence — is a performance proxy ([Khan blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)); LearnLM's seven-benchmark suite explicitly has no learning-outcome measure ([Jurenka et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12687v3)), and no pedagogy benchmark has been validated against learning gains.

The defensible evaluation ladder has four rungs, and only the top two count as learning evidence. Rung one is an offline pedagogy regression suite run on every prompt or model change: answer-leakage gates, the EduFrameTrap-style pressure test cases (a student asserting a wrong claim confidently is an explicit fixture), hint-ceiling compliance under adversarial personas, and rubric-scored transcripts — the supervisor paper ran 500+ deterministic unit tests plus an auditor model at under a dollar per loop ([arXiv 2608.12292](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12292)). Rung two is online A/B on next-item correctness and on a retention proxy such as first-attempt correctness on the next FSRS review of the same concept. Rung three is pre/post on a matched unit with a **tool-removed** post-test, the Bastani practice-versus-exam split. Rung four is delayed or proctored retention, weeks later; no LLM-tutor RCT has yet reported one. Two design tricks from the literature make small-N measurement feasible: Lindsey's within-student randomization of concepts to conditions (each student is their own control, which is how d ≈ 1.4 was detectable with 179 students), and normalized gain on a concept inventory as the instrument — remembering that the 69-student Khanmigo study was underpowered for a between-group effect. The remodel's N7 outcome eval should log, per concept, the evidence chain from first probe to delayed review and report learning gain as the change in tool-removed check accuracy at ≥ 2 days, not the change in P(known).

## The video got the loop right and the evidence layer wrong

Eero Alvar's "How I Use AI to Learn Things" (repo: [amosblomqvist/learn](https://github.com/amosblomqvist/learn)) describes a probe → plan → teach system: a multiple-choice quiz tool that "binary searches" the edge of knowledge, a plan reasoned out with verification sub-agents and drawn as a Mermaid graph, and teaching in discrete reasoning steps with periodic feedback quizzes and practice problems, persisted as Markdown in Obsidian (comment [10]'s reconstruction). Against the evidence, it got four things right. The three-phase loop *is* VanLehn's outer loop plus an inner loop, and a top commenter correctly named the probe as the ZPD found by adaptive testing within a learner model ([2], 586 likes). "One reasoning step at a time" ([27]) is learner-paced segmenting, d = 0.45 on transfer, and matches the brevity rule in the RCT that beat active learning. The author's stated position that "multiple choice in this system is primarily a measurement instrument, not the learning instrument" and that "longer, harder problems is the way to go" for practice ([58] reply) is exactly the probe-versus-check split the CAT and retrieval literatures support. And moving the struggle from logistics to material is the right target: the assistance dilemma says withholding too much wastes time and giving too much produces shallow learning ([Koedinger & Aleven 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-007-9049-0)).

It got or missed five things that the evidence treats as load-bearing, and the comment thread supplied most of them. No spaced review: the author conceded it is "currently entirely absent" ([19], author reply); the missing piece is worth d ≈ 0.9–1.4 in a semester course and the commenter's request to "maintain the state of previous study sessions" is the learner brief. Multiple choice as the only check: at least nine commenters ([26], [33], [53], [70], [115], [160], [193], [233], and the [31] request to write out reasoning) objected that MC can be "cheesed" by elimination, gives the illusion of mastery, and never shows the student struggling; the evidence agrees — short answer beats MC with feedback (d = 0.41), self-explanation g = 0.55, teach-back g = 0.56, and right-answer-wrong-reason is a documented false positive. The fix is the evidence-channel table above, with tier-2 reasons and free-response or teach-back before mastery is credited, and [26]'s "open a new instance and explain it to the AI from a blank prompt" is literally the teach-back check. No grounding: commenters asked for anchoring in textbooks and the learner's own sources ([89], [231], [232]) and warned about hallucination and Kimi's error rate ([143], [268]); Bastani's 51% figure and the Kestin/Bastani designs make a verified answer key the precondition for hint-not-answer, which is where Sapling's course-material RAG and syllabus become the differentiator. No memory and no goal: [116] wanted an agent that is "not a blank slate every time," [191] wanted goals rather than topics, [12] observed that a total novice gets everything wrong and leaves the system "without a reference point for calibration" — the CAT floor rule, the "I don't know" answer suggested in [12]'s reply, and ALEKS's course-informed prior are the answers. And the video's founding premise — that trust in a single teacher is what makes learning work — is not what the RCTs show; what separates +1 SD from −0.2 SD is guardrails and grounding, not the number of teachers, and [149]/[160]/[262]'s counter that a student should *not* blindly trust the teacher is closer to the sycophancy and confusion-induction evidence.

Other commenter suggestions the evidence supports: [3]'s call to manage "curiosity, frustration, satisfaction" in different quantities is the D'Mello state machine, and its "flashcards for facts, exercises for repetition" split is the fact-versus-concept scheduler split; [173]'s weekly recap quizzes over everything learned are the successive-relearning boosters; [198]'s "questions the learner should have asked but didn't" is a metacognitive prompt of the kind Fan et al. found missing; [222]'s point that revisiting old material reveals connections is spacing plus interleaving; [5]'s wish for a benchmark measured by learning outcomes on secret exams is exactly rung four of the evaluation ladder and the gap every current benchmark has; [59]'s insistence that modality should fit the topic "not the person" is precisely what the learning-styles meta-analysis concludes; [220]'s stat-levelling is safe only as mastery-tied levels, never a leaderboard. Two suggestions the evidence does not back: the Socratic-only pleas ([3] reply, [97]) have no learning-outcome RCT and pure questioning degrades in long dialogues, so graded assistance beats it; and [110]'s "multiple outlets so you don't overfit to the style" has no support once learning styles are removed. The skeptics ([16], [26], [35], [53], [153], [162]) were partly right for the wrong reasons: dependence and the illusion of learning are real (Bastani, Fan, Rismanchian), but the remedy is not "read a book" — the same trials show a guardrailed tutor beating a good classroom.

## Prioritized recommendations for Sapling

The list is ordered by evidence strength times how much of today's system it repairs, and maps onto the remodel's nodes where it can.

1. **Put a deterministic answer-withholding policy in code, grounded in a verified answer key** (N10/N11 first, before anything else). Every check item carries a reference answer and a short "common wrong reasons" block generated at ingest and reviewed against course material; the tutor prompt gets hint-not-answer; a code layer computes the help ceiling from learner state, never from student text; a solution detector strips leaks; full solution only after two genuine attempts on practice items. Evidence: Bastani 2025 and Kestin 2025 [classroom RCT]; supervisor architecture [benchmark]. This is the difference between harm and gain.

2. **Add a graded check tool to the tutor and make it the only strong evidence** (arrows 14–19). Free response and teach-back graded per rubric item against the reference with confidence; MC plus a one-sentence reason; "I don't know" as a first-class answer. Grader never solves the item itself. Evidence: grading κ ≈ 0.7 [lab]; retrieval practice g ≈ 0.5 [classroom meta-analysis].

3. **Replace the scalar mastery score with per-channel BKT** (N5/N6). Fixed priors L0 ≈ 0.3–0.4, T ≈ 0.1–0.2, G/S per channel from the table, G + S < 1 enforced; decay toward L0 at read; one-hop asymmetric propagation; proficient ≥ 0.95, mastered ≥ 0.98 with ≥ 3 strong observations; append-only evidence log; nightly pyBKT refit only past 50 students × 15 opportunities, keyed on the abstract concept. Remove the tutor's −0.1..+0.3 self-judged delta and the flat ±0.03 quiz delta; the chat channel gets G = S = 0.3. Evidence: BKT equations and bounds [model + Cognitive Tutor practice]; 0.98 threshold [classroom quasi-experiment, 2025]; small-data failure of neural KT [benchmark].

4. **Connect an FSRS-6 scheduler** (N12) with r = 0.90 (0.85 large sets, 0.95 exam week), format-aware ratings, |R − 0.33| ordering over budget, a 10–15 min/day budget, the 3-then-3 successive-relearning state machine, and review answers routed through the same grader. Flashcards get facts, the review quiz gets concepts, siblings get interleaved after acquisition. Evidence: Lindsey 2014 d = 1.42 [classroom]; Rawson & Dunlosky [classroom]; FSRS defaults [algorithm docs, Anki data only].

5. **Write a real end-of-session close and read it back** (arrows 11, 18): model-written summary, self-evaluation prompt, if-then plan for next session, all injected as a bounded learner brief rebuilt only when the step changes (N9). Evidence: implementation intentions d = 0.65 [meta-analysis]; Fan 2025 [RCT]; Khan +3.4% [vendor].

6. **Build the probe with CAT rules**: 4–6 items per skill, 50–70% target, hard cap 8–12, floor rule (3 misses at easiest → teach lowest prerequisite), seeded from quiz history and syllabus. Evidence: CAT stopping rules [psychometric]; ALEKS assessment validity AUROC 0.86–0.89 [production data]. Item difficulty is uncalibrated, so treat selection as heuristic.

7. **Build the course map with prerequisite edges and plan at the outer fringe** (N3/N8): 3–5 new independent concepts per session, 1–2 if coupled; reviews → new → interleaved siblings; the student approves the path but not step size or hints. Evidence: KST [theory + production]; CLT [theory]; learner-control g = 0.05 [meta-analysis].

8. **Encode the teaching step as a state machine**: novice = bounded attempt → worked example in chunks → near-transfer with step hints; developing = problem-first with hints; proficient = problem plus verification. Every turn ≤ 3–5 sentences, one signaled element, ends with the student's move. Feedback = task + process + self-regulation, after an attempt, no praise, no comparison, no mid-loop grades. Evidence: expertise reversal [meta-analysis 2025]; productive failure [meta-analysis]; segmenting/signaling [meta-analyses]; Wisniewski 2020 [meta-analysis].

9. **Implement the slip/misconception/novice rule and store misconceptions on the learner model**, feeding the class rollup above n ≥ 5 (N4). Evidence: FCI design principles [instrument validation]; Lehman 2013 [lab].

10. **Stand up the evaluation ladder from day one of step 2** (N7): leakage and sycophancy fixtures in the existing evals; a retention proxy (first-attempt correctness on next review); a tool-removed unit post-test; within-student randomization of concepts to scheduler variants. Report learning gain as delayed tool-removed accuracy, never as ΔP(known).

11. **Motivation surface last, and cheaply**: mastery-tied levels, weekly streak with freeze, no leaderboards, error-as-information framing embedded in feedback, autonomy over goal/topic/pacing only, and rooms that run the Peer Instruction cycle with aggregate (never ranked) class signals. Evidence: Hanus & Fox [college quasi-experiment]; Sailer & Homner [meta-analysis]; Crouch & Mazur, Smith 2009 [classroom].

12. **Do not build**: a learning-style onboarding field; a standalone mindset module; deep knowledge tracing; full Bayesian-network inference; a Socratic-only mode; an LLM-in-the-loop learner model.

## Open questions the evidence cannot answer

Several decisions above rest on inference, and Sapling's own data will have to settle them. No study calibrates BKT guess/slip for LLM-graded evidence; the channel table is derived from label-accuracy numbers, and the first refit will tell whether teach-back evidence is closer to free response or to chat. No published scheduler maps check format onto FSRS stability, and FSRS's 21 defaults come from self-graded Anki cards; whether an LLM-graded "explain the concept" retrieval reproduces the successive-relearning stability gain is untested. Interleaving's effect on conceptual, non-mathematical college content (history, biology definitions) is the weak zone of the meta-analysis. The 85% band has no human RCT behind it. No RCT pits Socratic-only against graded-assistance LLM tutoring on learning outcomes, none compares fine-tuning with prompting on outcomes, and none reports delayed retention for any LLM tutor. Bastani is high-school; the only college-scale guardrail evidence is Kestin's one-week physics unit. LLM misconception diagnosis from free text has no accuracy figure, only MCQ distractor mapping. Group fairness of LLM grading has direction but no magnitude in the sources. And nothing in the literature documents the specific composition proposed here — per-channel BKT with FSRS decay and one-hop DAG propagation — so it is a synthesis of ALEKS, MATHia, Knewton and Lindsey rather than a cited design, and should be treated as a hypothesis to be measured on rung three of the ladder before it is trusted.

## Conclusion

The literature's deepest lesson for Sapling is that the learning system and the chat are different things. Every configuration that produced measured learning — Cognitive Tutor, ALEKS, AutoTutor, Lindsey's COLT, Kestin's tutor — kept the pedagogy in a deterministic layer (what to ask, what counts as evidence, when to review, how much help is allowed) and used the language surface only to deliver it. Sapling's current architecture inverts that: the model decides what it thinks the student knows, and nothing outside the model checks it. Moving the state and the policy out of the prompt is not a modelling nicety; it is what turns a tool that lifts practice scores into one that lifts exams.

The second lesson is that the missing pieces are cheap and the effect sizes are large in the right order. A three-line Bayes update, a power-law forgetting curve with known defaults, a threshold selector, and a rubric grader that sees the answer key cost almost nothing to run and are the components behind the d ≈ 0.8–1.4 classroom results, whereas better prose, more personality, and a bigger model are behind none of them. Sapling's real advantage over the video's system and over generic study modes is that it already holds the course material, the syllabus, and the student's notes — the verified key, the goal, and the prior that every successful design needed and that a blank-slate chatbot cannot have.
