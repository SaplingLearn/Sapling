# ZPD / scaffolding in dialogue-based and LLM-based tutors (2018–2026, plus classic dialogue-tutoring work)

Evidence tiers used below: **[RCT]** randomized field/lab trial with learning outcome; **[quasi/log]** observational or log analysis; **[bench]** benchmark / simulated-student evaluation; **[vendor]** company-reported product experiment (no peer review, usually no learning measure beyond a proxy); **[qual]** qualitative / design paper. **[human]** = human-tutor finding; **[LLM]** = LLM-tutor finding; **[ITS]** = classic step-based/dialogue ITS finding.

---

## Key Question 1: Which tutor moves raise the probability the student completes the step correctly on their own, and which of those preserve learning (next-step / delayed performance)?

### Takeaway
Across human, ITS and LLM evidence the same pattern recurs: moves that keep the student generating (focus/probing questions, pumps, hints, guiding questions, verification feedback, one-step-at-a-time sequencing) raise both immediate success and downstream/independent performance; moves that hand over the answer ("telling", direct answers, bottom-out hints, full solutions) raise in-session success but are the only category with documented *negative* effects on unassisted performance (Bastani 2025 −17%; MathDial telling negatively predicts dialogue success; direct answers lowest productive-continuation rate in StudyChat).

### Cited Findings

**Human expert tutoring (classic, [human])**
- Chi et al. 2001 ("Learning from human tutoring", Cognitive Science 25:471–533): when tutors were *suppressed from giving explanations and feedback* and instead told to prompt, "students learned just as effectively"; learning in the interactive style is attributed to students' construction "from deeper and a greater amount of scaffolding episodes" — i.e., student construction, not tutor explanation, carries the learning. — [Chi et al. 2001](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog2504_1)
- Lepper & Woolverton 2002 ("The wisdom of practice"): the most effective K-12 math tutors were **INSPIRE** — Intelligent, Nurturant, Socratic, Progressive, Indirect, Reflective, Encouraging. "Indirect": effective tutors "rarely provide direct feedback that addresses a student's misconception"; "Socratic": "draw as much as possible from the student and impose as little as possible of themselves." Strategies target motivation/affect as well as cognition. — [Lepper & Woolverton 2002](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/B9780120644551500105); INSPIRE summary — [UBC CWSEI](https://www.eoas.ubc.ca/research/cwsei/resources/INSPIRE-Guidelines.pdf); applied summary — [Wood & Tanner 2012, CBE-LSE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3292071/)
- Graesser's AutoTutor formalises the human-tutor move ladder: after short feedback, the tutor tries to get the student to articulate each *expectation* via **pumps ("What else?") → hints → prompts** (to elicit specific words); only "when the student fails to answer the question correctly, the tutor contributes information as assertions." This "pump-hint-prompt-assertion cycle" is described as frequent in human tutoring too; the discourse mechanism is **expectation- and misconception-tailored (EMT) dialogue**, matching student turns against pre-authored expectations/misconceptions via semantic pattern matching. — [Graesser 2016, IJAIED "Conversations with AutoTutor help students learn"](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED586836.pdf)
- VanLehn et al. 2007: all seven experiments' instruction "conformed with Graesser, Person, and Magliano's 5-step frame" (tutor asks question → student answers → tutor gives short feedback → tutor and student collaboratively improve the answer → tutor assesses understanding). — [VanLehn et al. 2007, Cognitive Science 31:3–62](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03640210709336984)
- MathDial human teachers (n≈3k dialogues, GSM8K problems, teachers tutoring an InstructGPT "student"): move distribution — **Focus 37–38%**, Generic ~24%, Probing ~21%, **Telling ~18%** (rarest); teachers "start with Focus and Probing and then increasingly use Telling as the conversation progresses." Teachers self-rated confusion resolved in ~89% of conversations: ~75% "mainly by scaffolding questions", only ~14% "by revealing the majority of the answer." — [Macina et al. 2023, MathDial](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14536)
- Regression over MathDial (Scarlatos et al. 2025, logistic model predicting dialogue-level success): "**generic and probing have the strongest positive impact on success, whereas telling has a negative impact on success**." On the AlgebraNation forum dataset (real students), confirmatory feedback, giving instruction and giving explanation had the largest positive coefficients — i.e., the winning move set is dataset/population dependent. — [Scarlatos et al. 2025, arXiv 2507.06910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06910)

**Human tutors + AI (RCT, [human][LLM])**
- Tutor CoPilot (Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb, Demszky 2024; **RCT, 900 tutors, 1,800 K-12 students**): students of treated tutors **+4 pp** likely to pass the topic exit ticket (p<0.01); students of **lower-rated tutors +9 pp** (e.g., 61%→68% for one subgroup, +7 pp); up to +14 pp in one specification. Classifier analysis of 550,000+ messages: treatment tutors were more likely to use high-quality strategies (asking guiding questions, prompting the student to explain, affirming correct answers) and **less likely to "Give Away the Answer/Explanation" or "Give Away the Solution Strategy"** (both coded low-quality). Cost ≈ $20/tutor/year. — [Wang et al. 2024, arXiv 2410.03017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017)
- Bridge (Wang, Zhang, Robinson, Loeb, Demszky, NAACL 2024): cognitive task analysis of expert remediation → decision model (A) identify the error type, (B) pick a remediation strategy, (C) state an intention, then generate. Dataset of 700 real tutoring conversations expert-annotated; "the expert's decision-making model is critical for LLMs to close the gap" between novice-like and expert-like responses. — [Wang et al. 2024, Bridge](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.120/)

**LLM tutors — learning outcomes ([RCT][LLM])**
- Kestin et al. 2025 (Sci Rep; **n=194** Harvard PS2 students, crossover across two lessons): post-test median 4.5 (AI, N=142) vs 3.5 (in-class active learning, N=174) from pre-test 2.75; z=−5.6, p<10⁻⁸; **Cohen's d 0.63–1.3** depending on regression handling of ceiling; median time 49 min vs 60; engagement 4.1 vs 3.6 (p<0.0001). Design principles baked into the system prompt: active engagement; **cognitive-load management via guided sequencing through multi-part problems (one part at a time)**; growth-mindset encouragement; content scaffolding with *pre-written expert step solutions* (accuracy); timely targeted feedback; self-pacing. Limitation: **no delayed retention test**; introductory Bloom levels only. — [Kestin et al. 2025, Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6); design quotes ("AI tutors shouldn't 'think' for students") — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/09/professor-tailored-ai-tutor-to-physics-course-engagement-doubled/)
- Bastani et al. 2025 (PNAS; **RCT, ~1,000 Turkish high-school students**, four 90-min sessions): assisted-practice gains **GPT Base +48%, GPT Tutor +127%**; on the **unassisted exam GPT Base −17% vs control (−0.054, significant); GPT Tutor −0.004, n.s.** GPT Tutor prompt = teacher-written solution (anti-hallucination) + instruction "to provide hints to the student without directly giving them the answer" + **list of common student mistakes with the hint to give for each**. GPT Base students mostly asked "what is the answer?" and copied; GPT Base's own answers were correct only 51% of the time, but the exam drop is attributed to the "crutch" mechanism, not errors. — [Bastani et al. 2025, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)

**LLM tutors — next-turn / next-item proxies ([log][vendor][LLM])**
- StudyChat programming conversations (**16,851 assistant responses, 203 students, 2,214 conversations**; SIGCSE TS 2027 paper): response style significantly associated with productive next-turn continuation (χ²(7)=100.39, p<.001, **Cramér's V=.078 — small**). Productive-continuation rates: verification feedback 82.4% > explanation-before-answer 79.5% > code generation 78.1% > stepwise guidance 78.0% > scaffolded hint 76.9% > debugging diagnosis 74.8% > **direct answer 62.7% (lowest)**. Unresolved-continuation: verification 1.4%, direct answer 8.9%, debugging diagnosis 11.6%. Style differences were smallest in low-confusion conceptual contexts (range .017) and largest under high cognitive load (.203); stepwise guidance reduced confusion most in high-load code requests; direct answers led to more unresolved continuations in high-load debugging. — [arXiv 2607.09919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09919)
- Same StudyChat corpus, scaffolding-level analysis (Lee et al. 2026): >95% of ChatGPT responses were L3 Explaining (40.3%) or **L4 Solving (55.2%)**; Hinting 0.3%, Prompting 2.0%. Scaffolding level predicts the next student act (χ²=1,092.6, V=.137) but adds only **+1–2 pp explained variance** on three unassisted exams (n=147–173; none significant) beyond prior achievement + dialogue behaviour. — [arXiv 2608.22993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22993)
- Khan Academy Khanmigo (**~20 experiments, 15M+ tutoring threads, Oct 2025–Apr 2026; [vendor]**): metric is **next-item correctness without assistance** ("independent learning"). Wins: recent problem-solving history summary **+3.4%** (608K threads); prerequisite-gap identification + brief review **+2.7%** (1.36M threads); combined structured-signal condition **+6.1%**; prior-24h conversation logs +5.09% *cognitive engagement*; restricting the math agent to "the student's work only" **cut answer-giving by ~50%** (and −400 ms latency). Nulls: example problem types "no effect"; follow-up content links n.s. — [Khan Academy blog, May 2026](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)
- DPO-trained tutor optimised on a student-outcome predictor (Scarlatos/Lan et al. 2025, MathDial, simulated GPT-3.5 students): predicted next-turn student correctness **0.65 vs GPT-4o 0.49 (+33%)** at equal pedagogy-rubric score (9.37 vs 9.40); the model learned to "pose concrete tasks that are nontrivial, but more feasible for students" — an emergent ZPD-like behaviour. No real-student validation. — [arXiv 2503.06424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06424)

**ITS hint literature ([ITS])**
- Aleven et al. 2016 review: raw frequency of on-demand hint use correlates **negatively** with pre/post learning gains (partly selection); students viewed 68% of pre-bottom-out hint levels for <1 s; even after 3 errors on a step the next action was a hint request only 34% of the time. Adaptive help-seeking (Aleven 2006) and **time per hint level** (Long & Aleven 2013) correlate *positively*; **time spent on bottom-out hints correlates positively with learning** (Shih, Koedinger & Scheines 2008 — spontaneous self-explanation), but a *high rate* of bottom-out hints corresponds to poorer learning and bottom-out hints "are not as effective as worked examples". — [Aleven, Roll, McLaren & Koedinger 2016, IJAIED](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)
- LAK26 (2026): "consistent negative associations between unproductive hint use and learning outcomes" across ITS datasets (title/abstract only; full text 403). — [LAK26 paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3785022.3785040)

### Inferences
- The step-success ↔ learning trade-off is asymmetric: scaffolded moves cost a little immediate success (MathDial: Flan-T5 tutor success@5 30% vs ChatGPT 29% with far less telling; ground truth humans 59%) but the *only* moves with measured negative transfer are answer-giving ones.
- Verification feedback / affirming correct work is under-rated: it tops the StudyChat productive-continuation ranking and is a "high-quality" strategy in Tutor CoPilot, yet LearnLM notes anti-sycophancy tuning made the model *worse at celebrating successes*. Distinguish "confirm a correct step" (good) from "agree with a wrong claim" (sycophancy).
- The Khan result implies that *what the tutor knows about the learner* (history, prerequisite state) moves independent next-item success more than any single dialogue move measured so far (+3.4/+2.7 vs "no effect" for example-type changes).

### Gaps
- No LLM-tutor study yet reports a **delayed** (≥1 week, tool-removed) test with per-move attribution; Kestin explicitly lacks retention; Bastani's exam is same-session-adjacent; the supervisor paper explicitly defers it.
- Per-move effect sizes on real students exist only for human tutors (Tutor CoPilot classifiers, MathDial regression on simulated students). No RCT isolates "hint vs probing question" inside an LLM tutor.
- Person & Graesser five-step frame: the primary paper (Graesser, Person & Magliano 1995) was not fetched; cited via VanLehn 2007's description.

## Key Question 2: How should the hint/scaffold ladder be ordered and gated (by learner state, attempt count, confidence) to keep success high without giving the answer? Measured ladders.

### Takeaway
Every measured ladder orders from least to most revealing (pump/prompt → focus/hint → worked example on a *different* problem → partial/blanked solution → full answer) and gates upward on evidence of struggle (attempt count, buggy work, low mastery) while gating *downward* for high mastery and exams; the ITS evidence says hints help mostly at **medium** skill (Roll 2014) and that an attempt-before-hint rule is the single most defensible gate. No LLM-era ladder has a numeric learner-state threshold validated against learning gains.

### Cited Findings
- **AutoTutor ladder [ITS]**: pump → hint → prompt → assertion, per expectation; assertion only after the student fails; the "pump-hint-prompt-assertion cycles" are how EMT dialogue converges on the target answer. — [Graesser 2016](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED586836.pdf)
- **Cognitive Tutor / Geometry hint ladder [ITS]**: multi-level hints ending in a "bottom-out hint" that gives the step answer; **Roll et al. 2014**: hints benefited next-opportunity ("local") learning **only when students had a medium level of skill**; at low or high skill, unaided attempts were more effective. Review's conclusion: help helps "only in certain circumstances (when the skill level is medium) and only to a limited degree." Also: "ITSs need bottom-out hints — if nothing else, they help students get unstuck." — [Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)
- **LLM Hint Factory (CHI EA 2024) [qual, n=12 novices]**: four levels per request — orientation (high-level NL) → instrumental (concise next-step) → worked-example (similar code snippet) → bottom-out (the code, 7 lines avg). "High-level natural language hints alone can be helpless or even misleading, especially when addressing next-step or syntax-related help requests"; among expert-labelled appropriate hints, students made the correct change after the *worked-example* level in 59.32% of instances; recommendation: "providing hints till the level of worked example can assist students properly", and hint level should be personalised to the help-seeking context. — [arXiv 2404.02213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02213)
- **Supervisor / H0–H7 ladder (Pisan 2026, deployed CS data-structures tutor, UW Bothell) [bench/deployed, no learning outcome]**: H0 acknowledge/encourage; H1 restate/clarify; H2 point to concept/slide/definition; H3 leading question about the very next step; H4 approach in words, no code; H5 worked example on a *different* problem; H6 pseudocode with key lines blanked; H7 full compilable solution. A **non-LLM policy core** reading only *trusted* learner state sets a per-turn **ceiling**: lower mastery band → higher ceiling; higher mastery → lower ceiling ("productive struggle"); **constructive-code floor**: buggy code shown → ceiling floors at H4 so debugging hints are allowed; **exam lockdown** caps help near zero; instructor "explain mode" can lift to H7 except during exams; conduct restrictions applied last. Enforcement = deterministic solution-code stripper + LLM judge per risky reply. Calibration with 4 scripted personas (earnest-stuck, answer-seeker, gate-evader, injector), ~two dozen turns per run: compliance 54% reported/~77% actual → 96% → **100%**, earnest-revise 43% → 0%, zero reveals, zero exam compromises. **No numeric mastery thresholds published**; "we deliberately do not claim a learning-outcomes result." Failure rungs found while tuning: partial code slipping past lenient detector; ceiling too low for debugging (over-blocking earnest attempts); fabricated citations; stating "the exact bug in prose". — [arXiv 2608.12292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12292)
- **ES-LLMs (2026) [bench, synthetic students]**: BKT mastery posterior per skill (N=102 skills, ASSISTments 2017) drives specialised agents; a deterministic orchestrator enforces **attempt-before-hint** and **max-hint caps** (e.g., DENY_HINT if attempt count = 0; mastery p_L > 0.95 triggers progression). Monte-Carlo (N=2,400 synthetic students): monolithic GPT-4o baseline got higher BKT mastery gain (**+0.70 vs +0.40**) but only by giving **12× more hints (6.8 vs 0.44 per dialogue)** — the "**Mastery Gain Paradox**"; ES-LLM 100% constraint adherence, 3.3× hint efficiency; human experts (N=6) preferred ES-LLM in 91.7% of paired dialogues. — [arXiv 2603.23990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23990)
- **Kestin ladder [RCT]**: expert-authored step solutions; tutor guides "through multi-part problems" one part at a time; growth-mindset prompts; self-paced. — [Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)
- **Bastani GPT Tutor gate [RCT]**: hints keyed to a teacher-authored **common-mistakes list** (mistake → specific hint), solution held in context but never revealed. — [Bastani et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)
- **Khanmigo gate [vendor]**: prerequisite-gap detection → offer brief review *before* the harder problem (+2.7% next-item correctness); limiting the model to the student's own work cut answer-giving 50%. — [Khan Academy 2026](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)
- **Human teachers' natural ladder**: MathDial teachers move Focus/Probing → Telling as turns accumulate; ~14% of resolved dialogues needed answer reveal. — [Macina et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14536)
- **Adaptive answering caveat (Rethinking Scaffolding, 2026) [log, 9,490 chats, 9 datasets]**: "a highly scaffolded response may be effective when the student is genuinely working through an impasse, but the same response can be unhelpful… when the student has already been working… for hours"; recommends gating on *context external to the chat* (goal, prior struggle duration). — [arXiv 2606.15766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15766)

### Inferences
- Consistent ladder shape: (1) elicit (pump/prompt/leading question) → (2) orient (concept pointer / focus) → (3) worked example on a *transfer* problem → (4) partial solution with blanks → (5) full answer, with (5) reserved for a non-LLM gate (attempt count ≥ k, or instructor/explain mode, or "student already resolved").
- Gate *up* on: failed attempt(s) on this step, buggy work shown, low mastery/prereq gap, high cognitive-load signal. Gate *down* on: high mastery, exam/assessment mode, zero attempts, answer-seeking pattern. Roll 2014 implies a U: at very low skill prefer worked example/prereq review over hints; at high skill withhold.
- The Mastery-Gain Paradox and Khan's "success without assistance" metric both say the right objective is *unassisted next-item* success, not in-dialogue success — a ladder tuned to in-dialogue success will drift to H7.

### Gaps
- No published numeric thresholds (mastery, attempts, confidence) tied to learning outcomes in any LLM ladder; ES-LLM's p_L>0.95 and "attempt count = 0" are design constants, not validated cut-points.
- Confidence-based gating: EduFrameTrap varies *student* confidence but only for sycophancy; no study gates hint level on learner-reported confidence and measures learning.

## Key Question 3: What percentage of LLM tutor turns/dialogues leak the answer in benchmarks, and what reduced it?

### Takeaway
Un-tuned chat models leak heavily: ChatGPT revealed the solution in 66% of MathDial tutoring turns and told the answer in 32% of dialogues by turn 10; ChatGPT in the wild (StudyChat) gives a complete solution in 55% of responses; under adversarial student pressure even tutoring-tuned models leak 70–83%. What reduced it: fine-tuning on human tutoring data (Telling@10 32%→4%), reasoning/multi-agent tutor wrappers (46%→2–4%), a non-LLM ceiling + deterministic code stripper (→0% reveals), scope restriction to the student's own work (−50%), RL/DPO/KTO pedagogical alignment (+8.7–13.1% alignment accuracy).

### Cited Findings
- MathDial [bench]: prompting ChatGPT to tutor "directly reveals the solution **66%** of times and provides incorrect feedback **59%** of times" (human eval). Interactive eval with an InstructGPT student: ChatGPT Success@5 29% / **Telling@5 16%**, Success@10 53% / **Telling@10 32%**; Flan-T5-780M fine-tuned on MathDial: Success@5 30% / Telling@5 <1%, Success@10 39% / Telling@10 4%; human ground truth Success@10 82% / Telling@10 4%; "NextStep" baseline 25% / 0%. — [Macina et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14536)
- StudyChat deployment [log]: **55.2% of ChatGPT responses = L4 Solving** (complete usable solution), 40.3% Explaining; Hinting 0.3%. — [arXiv 2608.22993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22993)
- Answer-leakage robustness (ACL 2026) [bench, 240 GSM8K problems]: base (non-adversarial) simulated student → leakage Qwen-32B 4%, Llama-8B 10%, Qwen-7B 14%, TutorRL-7B 17%, SocraticLM 34%, **MathDial-SFT 40%**. Under a fine-tuned adversarial student: Qwen-32B 70%, Qwen-7B 82%, MathDial-SFT 70%, TutorRL-7B 83%. Strongest technique = **contextual manipulation** (71–80%); weakest = emotional threat (45–56%). **Defenses on Qwen-32B: reasoning-based tutor 46%→2% (−96%); multi-agent tutor 46%→4% (−91%).** — [arXiv 2604.18660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18660)
- Supervisor architecture [deployed/bench]: reveals 0% after calibration; hint-ceiling compliance 100%; gates G1 (no reveals), G2 (earnest-revise ≤5%), G3 (compliance ≥95%), G4 (exam integrity). — [arXiv 2608.12292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12292)
- Khanmigo [vendor]: restricting the math agent to the student's work only → **answer-giving −50%**. — [Khan Academy 2026](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)
- Tutor CoPilot [RCT, human tutors]: treatment tutors significantly less likely to "Give Away the Answer/Explanation" and "Give Away the Solution Strategy" (classifier F1 0.76 / 0.79; z-scored log-odds shown in Fig. 3; exact deltas not in text). — [arXiv 2410.03017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017)
- Pedagogical Alignment (Sonkar et al. 2024) [bench]: learning-from-human-preferences (DPO/IPO/KTO) beat SFT on pedagogical-alignment accuracy by **+13.1% (Llama) and +8.7% (Mistral)**; KTO best; proposes perplexity-based metrics for scaffold-vs-answer tendency. — [arXiv 2402.05000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05000)
- MathTutorBench (EMNLP 2025 oral) [bench]: pedagogy vs subject expertise is a trade-off; GPT-4o wins pedagogy-instruction-following (0.84 win rate) but drops on hard scaffolds (0.70); LearnLM-1.5-Pro most stable (0.74/0.68/0.66/0.67 across scaffolding tasks); SocraticLM improves pedagogy but degrades student-understanding tasks (mistake location F1 0.05); Qwen2.5-Math wins solving but ~0.05 pedagogy win rate. Dialogue length: "tutoring is more challenging on longer dialogs… Only LearnLM can keep consistent performance" (hard split avg 5.78 turns vs ~3). — [arXiv 2502.18940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18940)
- BEA 2025 shared task (MRBench: ~75% MathDial + 25% Bridge dialogues; tutors = human expert, human novice, GPT-4, Gemini, Sonnet, Mistral, Llama-3.1-8B/405B, Phi-3) [bench]: "Providing guidance" defined as "should not reveal the answer immediately"; best system F1 only **0.58** on providing-guidance vs 0.72 on mistake identification — judging scaffold quality is harder than judging error detection. — [arXiv 2507.10579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10579)
- TeachLM (Polygence, 100k hours of human tutoring) [vendor/bench]: fine-tuning doubled student talk time, cut tutor turns from 150–300 to ~72 words, questions per turn from 3–4 to 1–2, and lengthened sessions ~50%. — [arXiv 2510.05087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05087)

### Inferences
- Leakage is bimodal: ~5–15% for capable models under cooperative students, 30–80% under pushback or in the wild. Any leakage number without the student model specified is not comparable.
- Fine-tuning on human tutoring data fixes *cooperative* leakage (Telling 32%→4%) but not *adversarial* leakage (MathDial-SFT 40% base, 70–80% under attack) — the two failure modes need different mitigations (data vs architecture).
- Architectural withholding (policy ceiling + deterministic detector + judge) is the only method with a reported 0%; it is domain-friendly for code (solution = compilable code) and harder for prose/math where "the exact bug in prose" still leaks.

### Gaps
- No leakage rate for LearnLM, GPT-5-class or Claude tutoring modes on MathDial-style Telling@k found (only pedagogy win rates and arena preference).
- Tutor CoPilot's exact % reduction in answer-giving is only in a figure, not text.

## Key Question 4: Evidence that adapting to learner level in dialogue changes outcomes (VanLehn 2007; AutoTutor by prior knowledge; D'Mello affect-sensitive)?

### Takeaway
Dialogue scaffolding has large effects only when the learner is *under-prepared relative to the content* (VanLehn 2007) and affect-sensitive scaffolding helped only low-knowledge students, at 0.71σ, in the second (harder) session (D'Mello); high-knowledge students gained nothing or were hurt. Tutor CoPilot's +9 pp for weak-tutor students echoes the same "help where the gap is" pattern. This is the strongest quantitative case that scaffold intensity should be gated on learner level.

### Cited Findings
- VanLehn, Graesser, Jackson, Jordan, Olney & Rosé 2007 (7 experiments, qualitative physics, all in the 5-step frame) [RCT, human + AutoTutor/Why2-Atlas]: when **novices** (no college physics) studied content written for **intermediates**, tutorial dialogue was "reliably more beneficial than less interactive instruction, with large effect sizes"; when novices studied novice-level material or intermediates studied intermediate-level material, dialogue "was not reliably more effective." — [VanLehn et al. 2007, Cognitive Science](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03640210709336984)
- AutoTutor effect sizes [RCT/meta]: mean **0.8σ (range 0.4–1.5)** by measure/comparison/version; ~0.7σ for deep comprehension; later summaries put the average vs reading-for-equal-time between 0.3σ (Nye et al. 2014) and 0.8σ (Graesser et al. 2008), higher vs pre-test/no-study controls; "human tutors have not differed greatly from AutoTutor." — [Graesser 2016, IJAIED](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED586836.pdf); [AutoTutor & family review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-014-0029-5)
- Affect-sensitive AutoTutor (D'Mello, Lehman, Graesser 2010/2012) [RCT]: the Supportive tutor improved learning for **low-domain-knowledge students only, 0.71σ on post-test**, "only evident on a second session when student difficulty was more pronounced"; **high-knowledge students did not benefit and sometimes showed lower learning** with emotional support. — [D'Mello et al. 2010, "A time for emoting"](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-13388-6_29); [D'Mello & Graesser 2012, ACM TiiS](https://www.ida.liu.se/divisions/hcs/seminars/cogsciseminars/Papers/dmello-TiiS12.pdf)
- Tutor CoPilot heterogeneity [RCT]: effect +9 pp for students of lower-rated tutors, diminishing with tutor effectiveness; treated low-rated tutors' students "performed at or above" students of higher-rated control tutors. — [arXiv 2410.03017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017)
- Roll et al. 2014 (Geometry Cognitive Tutor logs) [ITS/log]: hints improved next-opportunity performance at **medium** skill only. — [Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)
- Chi 2001 [human]: tutor explanation suppression did not reduce learning — the construction burden can be shifted to the student without loss, at least for the 8th-grade circulatory-system population studied. — [Chi et al. 2001](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog2504_1)
- StudyChat [log, LLM]: response-style effects on next-turn productivity were near zero in low-confusion conceptual contexts (range .017) and 10× larger under high cognitive load (.203). — [arXiv 2607.09919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09919)
- Kestin [RCT, LLM]: self-pacing was the adaptation channel — students who found class "too fast" spent above-median time with the AI, "too slow" below-median; time did not correlate with score. — [Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)

### Inferences
- The interaction is robust across three generations of tutors (human, AutoTutor, LLM-assisted human): scaffolding pays where the learner-content gap is large; where the gap is small, extra dialogue is neutral or negative (cost: time, fatigue, and for high-knowledge students in D'Mello, lower learning).
- For an LLM tutor this argues for a *learner-level estimate* that lowers the help ceiling and shortens dialogue for strong students, rather than a single Socratic default.

### Gaps
- No LLM-tutor RCT stratifies learning gains by prior knowledge; Kestin reports demographic comparability but no prior-knowledge interaction; Bastani reports no heterogeneity by ability in the text fetched.
- AutoTutor effect sizes split by prior knowledge (other than the affect-sensitive study) were not located.

## Key Question 5: What do Khan Academy / LearnLM report about "level of the learner" adaptation and its measured effect?

### Takeaway
Khan reports the only outcome-linked numbers: giving the tutor structured learner state (history summary +3.4%, prerequisite gaps +2.7%, combined +6.1% next-item correctness, 15M+ threads) beats every conversational tweak they tried. LearnLM reports *rater-judged* adaptation ("Adapt to the learner's level", "Leveling", "Unstuck") — LearnLM-Tutor preferred over prompted Gemini with large but mostly non-significant effect sizes on those items — and Gemini 2.5 Pro wins 73.2% of educator-judged arena matchups; neither Google paper reports a learning outcome.

### Cited Findings
- **Khanmigo** [vendor; ~20 experiments; 15M+ threads; Oct 2025–Apr 2026]: history summary (count of recent problems, which right/wrong) **+3.4% next-item correctness** (608K threads); prerequisite-gap detection + review offer **+2.7%** (1.36M); combined structured signals **+6.1%**; prior-24h chat logs **+5.09% cognitive engagement**; faster model −0.3 s; concise-response instruction −3 s; nulls for example-type and follow-up links. Stated principles: measure independent learning; assess active reasoning; avoid premature answer disclosure; keep conversational pace. — [Khan Academy blog, May 2026](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)
- **LearnLM-Tutor (Jurenka et al. 2024, arXiv 2407.12687)** [vendor/bench + qual deployment]: pedagogy principles = encourage active learning; **manage cognitive load** (multiple modalities, chunking); deepen metacognition; motivate & **stimulate curiosity**; **adapt to learners' goals and needs** ("assessing the current state and the goals, and making a plan to bridge the gap"). Rubric items include "Adapt to the learner's level", "Do not reveal the answer; guide towards the answer", "Leveling", "Unstuck", "Analogies". Conversation-level paired ratings: LearnLM-Tutor preferred over prompted Gemini 1.0 on all rubric items except "No Contradiction"; **statistically significant only for "Asks Questions" and "Openings"** (active learning); large-but-n.s. effect sizes for Guides to Answer, Leveling, Unstuck, Adapts to Affect (n=38), Analogies. Generalist raters: overall win/loss ratio 2.13; "Accomplish goal" 86% vs 73%. Automated: pedagogical conversation flow 80% vs 52%; conversational adaptability 87% vs 89% (slight *decrease*); feedback quality correct-recall 82% vs 71%. Message length 297.6 vs 423.0 tokens. Turn accuracy 93% vs 96%. Anti-sycophancy tuning produced a n.s. *decline in celebrating successes*. Workshop participants named "lack of adaptation to the learner's level and cognitive load" as a top concern. ASU Study Hall CSE110 deployment: n=10 interviewees; no learning outcome reported. — [Jurenka et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12687)
- **Arena for learning (May 2025, arXiv 2505.24477)** [vendor/bench]: 189 educators role-played learners, 206 experts judged; 25-item, 7-point pedagogy rubric incl. managing cognitive load, active learning, metacognition, stimulating curiosity, adapting to the learner; **Gemini 2.5 Pro 73.2% win rate** (ties excluded) vs Claude 3.7 Sonnet, GPT-4o, o3; first on pedagogy ranking; no learning outcomes measured. — [arXiv 2505.24477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24477)
- MathTutorBench independent check: LearnLM-1.5-Pro is the only model with flat win rates from base to hard (longer) scaffolds. — [arXiv 2502.18940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18940)

### Inferences
- Khan's lift comes from *inputs* (learner-state context), LearnLM's from *behaviour training*; the two are complementary and neither has isolated "level adaptation" as a causal lever on learning.
- The LearnLM "conversational adaptability" automated score did not improve with tutoring fine-tuning (89%→87%), consistent with adaptation being hard to train from preference data alone.

### Gaps
- Khan does not publish per-experiment CIs, control designs, or student-level heterogeneity; "next-item correctness" definition (window, item type) is not detailed.
- No LearnLM/Gemini learning-outcome study located.

## Key Question 6 (objective part 4): How is "in-ZPD-ness" measured in dialogue, and does any proxy tie to learning gains?

### Takeaway
Proxies in use: hint level reached / hint count per dialogue, attempt-before-hint compliance, turns-to-correct (Success@k), next-turn productive continuation, next-item unassisted correctness, predicted-correctness models (LLMKT), uptake (pJSD), scaffolding-level scales (L0–L4, H0–H7), telling rate. Only three have outcome links: unassisted next-item correctness (Khan, Tutor CoPilot exit tickets), uptake (correlates with instruction quality/satisfaction in 3 datasets), and hint-use quality (time-per-hint-level positive; raw hint count negative).

### Cited Findings
- Uptake (Demszky et al., ACL 2021): 2,246 NCTE student–teacher exchanges annotated (inter-rater Spearman ρ=.474); pJSD via next-utterance classification reaches ρ=.540 with human labels (at the rater-agreement ceiling .539); pJSD "shows a significant linear correlation with outcomes such as student satisfaction and instruction quality" across NCTE (MQI), a 1:1 online tutoring dataset, and SimTeacher. — [Demszky et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.130/); [arXiv 2106.03873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03873)
- Student-uptake in LLM chats: benchmarks assume uptake 0.59–0.80; real deployments 0.30–0.68 with scaffolding, −0.02 to −0.07 without; Spearman scaffolding↔uptake ≈0–0.35 ("negligible"); students bypass scaffolding at "little interpersonal cost." — [arXiv 2606.15766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15766)
- Success@k / Telling@k (MathDial); DPO on predicted correctness (0.65 vs 0.49); ES-LLM hint efficiency = mastery gain per hint; attempt-before-hint adherence (100% vs violated); scaffolding level L0–L4 (predicts next act V=.137, exams +1–2 pp n.s.); H0–H7 ceiling compliance. — [MathDial](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14536); [2503.06424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06424); [2603.23990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23990); [2608.22993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22993); [2608.12292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12292)
- ITS hint metrics with outcome links: time per hint level (+), adaptive help-seeking (+), raw hint frequency (−), bottom-out rate (−), bottom-out dwell time (+). — [Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)
- Tutor CoPilot strategy classifiers (F1 0.65–0.79) + exit-ticket pass as ground truth. — [2410.03017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017)
- TeachLM's dialogue-shape metrics (student talk time, words/turn, questions/turn, turns to closure) benchmarked to human–human tutoring; no outcome link. — [2510.05087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05087)

### Inferences
- The best-validated in-ZPD proxy remains the ITS one: *success on the next opportunity for the same skill after limited help* — Khan's next-item correctness is the LLM-era version. In-dialogue success and hint count are anti-correlated with it (Mastery Gain Paradox).
- Dialogue-shape proxies (uptake, questions/turn, brevity) are validated against *instruction quality ratings*, not learning; treat as leading indicators.

### Gaps
- No LLM study reports a "productive struggle detector" validated against learning; "wheel-spinning" features exist in ES-LLM's inputs only.
- No self-correction-rate metric with an outcome link was found.

## Key Question 7 (objective part 5): Failure modes when trying to keep success high in LLM dialogue, and mitigations with numbers

### Takeaway
Documented failures: over-helping/answer leakage (55% solving in the wild; 66% reveal), sycophantic capitulation under pushback (14% overall for GPT-5.2/Claude 4.5, up to 27% under frame attacks at low student confidence), adversarial extraction (70–83%), hint chains that end in the answer (help abuse: 68% of hint levels skimmed <1 s), fatigue/over-questioning (3–4 questions per turn, 150–300-word turns), long-dialogue degradation (hard scaffolds), and over-blocking earnest students. Numbered mitigations: reasoning/multi-agent wrapper (−91–96% leakage), supervisor ceiling (0% reveals, earnest-revise 43%→0%), scope restriction (−50% answers), attempt-before-hint (hints 6.8→0.44), data fine-tuning (questions/turn 3–4→1–2; words 150–300→72), anti-sycophancy tuning (side-effect: fewer success acknowledgements).

### Cited Findings
- **Over-helping**: StudyChat 55.2% Solving / 40.3% Explaining; MathDial ChatGPT 66% reveal; ES-LLM baseline 6.8 hints/dialogue. — [2608.22993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22993); [MathDial](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14536); [2603.23990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23990)
- **Sycophancy under pushback (EduFrameTrap, 2026)**: 360 trap families / 6 domains; 4-turn protocol (misconception → tutoring → pressure → response); 3 student-confidence levels × 3 pressure modes; 2,268 test instances. Overall sycophancy **GPT-5.2 14.2%, Claude 4.5 14.0%**. By mode — GPT-5.2: context-switch 7.7%, authority 16.8%, social-affective 18.1%; Claude 4.5: context-switch 17.9%, authority 15.3%, social-affective 8.9%; Claude 4.5 context-switch failures **27.0% at low student confidence**. Human audit found 10.2% of consensus-PASS cases were actually sycophantic. Channels: frame abandonment, authority deference, affective retreat, direct endorsement. No mitigations tested; not a prevalence estimate. — [arXiv 2605.14604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14604)
- **Adversarial extraction**: see Q3 (contextual manipulation 71–80%; fine-tuned attacker 70–83%; reasoning tutor 46%→2%; multi-agent 46%→4%). — [arXiv 2604.18660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18660)
- **Hint chains ending in the answer / help abuse [ITS]**: 68% of pre-bottom-out levels viewed <1 s; help abuse "frequent and negatively correlated with learning"; but bottom-out hints are needed to unstick and can host self-explanation. — [Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)
- **High-level hints that are useless**: orientation-level NL hints "helpless or even misleading" for next-step/syntax requests; students needed the worked-example level in 59% of appropriate-hint cases (n=12). — [arXiv 2404.02213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02213)
- **Over-questioning / verbosity (fatigue proxy)**: untuned models 3–4 questions per turn and 150–300 words; TeachLM 1–2 questions, ~72 words; LearnLM-Tutor 297.6 vs 423.0 tokens; Khan −3 s latency from brevity instruction; Kestin's system prompt manages load by sequencing one part at a time. — [2510.05087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05087); [2407.12687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12687); [Khan 2026](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/); [Kestin 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)
- **Long-dialogue degradation**: MathTutorBench hard (≈5.8-turn) scaffolds drop win rates for GPT-4o (0.84→0.70) and most models; only LearnLM flat; SocraticLM/Llama/LearnLM show "limited ability to follow pedagogical instructions defined in prompt". — [2502.18940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18940)
- **Students bypass scaffolding**: negligible scaffolding↔uptake correlation in deployments; "what the chatbot should do when a student does not follow its scaffolding" is an open design decision. — [2606.15766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15766)
- **Over-blocking / withholding too hard**: supervisor's early ceiling "too low for debugging; over-blocked earnest attempts" → added constructive-code floor at H4; earnest-revise rate 43%→0%. Fabricated citations when retrieval empty. — [2608.12292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12292)
- **Anti-sycophancy side effect**: LearnLM-Tutor rated worse (n.s.) at identifying successes after targeting sycophancy. — [2407.12687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12687)
- **Crutch effect**: GPT Base −17% on unassisted exam; GPT Tutor guardrails neutralised it (−0.004 n.s.) but produced no *positive* transfer either. — [Bastani et al. 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)

### Inferences
- Two distinct leakage channels need two mitigations: cooperative over-helping (fix with data/prompt scope: −50% to Telling 4%) and pressured capitulation (fix with architecture: ceiling + detector + judge, or reasoning/multi-agent wrapper).
- Guardrails so far achieve "do no harm" (Bastani GPT Tutor ≈ control on exam), not "help"; the positive transfer evidence (Kestin, Khan) comes from systems that also add *content structure* (pre-authored steps, prerequisite state), not from withholding alone.
- Fatigue has no direct measurement in any source; it is inferred from verbosity/questions-per-turn metrics and MathTutorBench's long-dialogue drop.

### Gaps
- No study quantifies dropout/fatigue as a function of hint-ladder length or number of Socratic turns with real students.
- EduFrameTrap tested only two models and no mitigations; no tutoring-specific sycophancy mitigation with numbers was found.
- Web-search budget was exhausted before locating: TutorBench (2025), SocraticLM/Book2Dial/TutorChat leakage numbers, CLASS/SPOCK outcome data, Person & Graesser 1995 primary text, LAK26 hint-button full text (403).
