# Operationalize ZPD as hints-to-criterion, not 85%

Sapling should stop treating the zone of proximal development as a difficulty dial and start treating it as what Vygotsky actually defined: **the measured gap between what a student does alone and what they do with graded help**. The faithful, measurable form is Campione and Brown's graduated-prompt count — how many rungs of a fixed hint ladder a student needs to reach criterion — paired with the one downstream number every successful tutoring system converges on, **unassisted success on the next opportunity for the same concept**. Success rate is then a *controlled output* of that policy, not its objective, and it is phase-specific: probe at 50–62% expected success, acquire with scaffolds at 75–90% first-attempt-after-help success, practise unassisted at 65–85%, and schedule review to arrive near 90% predicted recall. No human study licenses a single number — the 85% rule is a gradient-descent theorem whose three human tests (all motor, N = 150–267) put the optimum at 50–70% success or found nothing; the two large academic RCTs disagree in direction (Math Garden's children learned most at the *easiest* 90% target because they did 52% more problems; 37,000 geography learners learned least at the 95% target and rated 65% "appropriate"); and the largest engagement dataset (80,000 sessions) finds engagement rising linearly with ease while per-item learning falls. What is consistent across every tier of evidence is the *shape* of the trade: raising success by **raising the learner** (prerequisite repair d ≈ 0.75, faded worked examples η² 0.08–0.27, sub-goal labels d = 0.44 on quizzes with course failure halved, retrieval warm-ups +10–21 points, contingent help after enforced independent time) preserves learning, while raising success by **shrinking the task** (pushed hints, bottom-out answers, answer-giving) inflates in-session accuracy by 48–127% and produces the only documented negative transfer (−17% on an unassisted exam, n ≈ 1,000). The re-verification pass confirmed 10 of 14 earlier headline numbers, corrected four (the segmenting split reverses on retention, the CAT "4–6 items" source is a depression-scale simulation, Kang's d = 0.41 is one feedback-dependent cell, Chi's "equal learning" is an n = 11 null), and recovered the Jansen and Papoušek per-condition numbers the earlier pass could not reach. The policy spec at the end turns this into rules, thresholds and log fields Sapling can ship against its existing `node_mastery_events` and `events` tables.

## Vygotsky defined a diagnostic gap, not a difficulty dial

The primary text is narrower than its ed-tech reception. Vygotsky's definition on page 86 of *Mind in Society* is that the ZPD "is the distance between the actual developmental level as determined by independent problem solving and the level of potential development as determined through problem solving under adult guidance or in collaboration with more capable peers" ([Vygotsky 1978, FAU scan](https://home.fau.edu/musgrove/web/vygotsky1978.pdf)) [theory; primary text]. The worked example is two ten-year-olds who both test at mental age eight alone; after assistance — "some might run through an entire demonstration and ask the children to repeat it, others might initiate the solution and ask the child to finish it, or offer leading questions" — one reaches a twelve-year-old's level and the other a nine-year-old's, and "this difference between twelve and eight, or between nine and eight, is what we call the zone of proximal development." Three features of that passage matter for Sapling. First, the zone is a *per-child measurement* obtained by contrasting unassisted with assisted performance, and the assistance is a graded menu (full demonstration, half-completed solution, leading questions). Second, the zone has an upper bound set by comprehension: "a person can imitate only that which is within her developmental level … if the teacher were to solve a problem in higher mathematics, the child would not be able to understand the solution no matter how many times she imitated it" (p. 88). Third, the instructional consequence is only that "the only 'good learning' is that which is in advance of development" (p. 89) — content already mastered is wasted, and "learning … creates the zone of proximal development" (p. 90). **Nothing in chapter 6 names a success-rate target, a "slightly too hard" dose, or a fading rule.**

Chaiklin's 2003 critique, the standard corrective, argues that "Vygotsky's concept of zone of proximal development is more precise and elaborated than its common reception" ([Cambridge chapter record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/vygotskys-educational-theory-in-cultural-context/zone-of-proximal-development-in-vygotskys-analysis-of-learning-and-instruction/1F8B412CF2A358988F0E7C2ABBCECBA3)) [theory]; as paraphrased in the one open secondary source, the ZPD is about maturing psychological *functions* across an age period, interventions "must be based on diagnostic procedures based on a learner's current state of development," and imitation "presupposes some understanding of the structural relations in a problem" ([Shabani, Khatib & Ebadi 2010, ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1081990.pdf)). The full chapter could not be retrieved in this pass, so the well-known "three assumptions" framing (generality, assistance, potential) stays flagged as unverified. The practical upshot is that "the ZPD for quadratic equations" is a category error in Vygotsky's terms; what ed-tech calls ZPD is really **Wood-style scaffolding plus Metcalfe's region of proximal learning**, and those literatures have effect sizes where Vygotsky has none.

Scaffolding is a separate, later construct. Wood, Bruner and Ross (1976; preschoolers on a block pyramid) listed six functions — recruitment, reduction in degrees of freedom, direction maintenance, marking critical features, frustration control, demonstration — and the same precondition as Vygotsky's imitation limit, that learners cannot benefit unless they "can understand the solution, even if they are not yet able to produce the solution independently" ([Margulieux summary of Wood et al. 1976](https://laurenmarg.com/2018/12/09/article-summary-wood-et-al-1976-role-of-tutoring-in-problem-solving/)) [lab; K-12; secondary source only, Wiley text unreachable]. Van de Pol, Volman and Beishuizen's decade review distilled the modern operational core: "contingency, fading, and transfer of responsibility are discerned in this review as the three key characteristics of scaffolding," with the honest coda that "the main challenge in scaffolding research appears to be its measurement" ([van de Pol et al. 2010, Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-010-9127-6)) [narrative review; K-12; no pooled effect]. The only classroom experiment on contingency is a warning rather than an endorsement: across **30 teachers and 768 students aged 12–15**, contingent support raised achievement only "in situations where independent working time was high (i.e., help was less frequent)"; where help was frequent, *low*-contingent support did better on both achievement and task effort, while contingency raised students' *appreciation* of support unconditionally ([van de Pol et al. 2015, ERIC EJ1071539](https://api.ies.ed.gov/eric/?search=id:EJ1071539&format=json)) [classroom quasi-RCT via teacher PD; contingency measured, not manipulated; K-12]. Being contingent is necessary; the timing and frequency of intervention is a separate lever that can cancel the benefit.

Dynamic assessment is the lineage that turned "assisted performance" into a number. Campione and Brown's graduated prompting is "a graduated prompting procedure where learners are offered increasingly more explicit hints in response to incorrect responses. All learners progress through the same menu of prompts or hints, varying with regard to the number of prompts required for task solution" ([Vanderbilt DA guide](https://researchguides.library.vanderbilt.edu/c.php?g=68756&p=444895)); the Resing group's version adds that "the number of prompts needed is taken as indication of a child's potential for learning" ([Resing group 2017, ERIC EJ1141388](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1141388.pdf)) [K-12; N = 120]. Ferrara, Brown and Campione showed with 8–11-year-olds that prompts-to-mastery separated average- from high-IQ and younger from older children, that groups "subsequently diverged in the number of prompts they needed as the transfer problems differed increasingly," and that "assistance required in learning proved quite stable across moderately related tasks" ([Ferrara et al. 1986, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/abstract/MED/3769602)) [lab; K-12]. The predictive-validity review of 24 studies found DA adds information over static tests, and — the detail that matters for a tutor — validity was "superior … when feedback is not contingent on student response," i.e., the *standardised* ladder predicts better than open clinical mediation ([Caffrey, Fuchs & Fuchs 2008, ERIC EJ796864](https://api.ies.ed.gov/eric/?search=title:%22predictive%20validity%20of%20dynamic%20assessment%22&format=json)) [meta-review; preschool–high school]. Swanson and Lussier's 30-study, 170-effect meta-analysis exists ([ERIC EJ644217](https://api.ies.ed.gov/eric/?search=title:%22selective%20synthesis%20of%20the%20experimental%20literature%20on%20dynamic%20assessment%22&format=json)) but its overall effect size (commonly quoted near d ≈ 1.0) was not verifiable from any open source and should not be cited as a number. The L2 tradition splits into interventionist (standardised, quantifiable) and interactionist (dialogic, no effect sizes) DA ([Language Testing in Asia 2022](https://languagetestingasia.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40468-022-00195-0)) [theory].

The correct measurable form therefore has three components, and they are ordered. **Assisted-minus-unassisted performance** is the definition. **Prompts-to-criterion on a fixed ladder** is its standardised, predictive-validated instrument, and it doubles as the learning-potential measure. **Unassisted success on the next opportunity** is the check that the zone actually moved ("what a child can do with assistance today she will be able to do by herself tomorrow"). A graduated hint ladder with a count of rungs used, followed by a later unassisted item on the same concept, is the most faithful operationalisation of the ZPD available and is the spine of the policy spec below. Vygotsky's two-sided constraint — content already doable alone is wasted, content not understood even when demonstrated is also wasted — adds a *comprehension* test the success-rate framing lacks: the zone is where a worked solution is comprehensible but not yet reproducible.

## Every optimal-success number is a model, a convention or a motor task

Three conclusions follow. First, **information and motivation pull in opposite directions and the compromise is known**: maximum-information selection gives ~50% success ([Eggen & Verschoor 2006](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/0146621606288890)) [psychometric], but that costs measurable fear of failure (Ortner) and the assessment world's negotiated answer is 0.62 (PISA). Second, **acquisition speed and dose favour higher success while retention and transfer favour informed, effortful attempts** — and the primaries already state the reconciliation. Renkl's fading benefit disappears when errors-during-practice is covaried, i.e. fading works *because* it keeps error rate low during acquisition ([Renkl et al. 2002](http://www.davidlewisphd.com/courses/EDD8121/readings/2002-Renkl_et_al.pdf)) [lab + classroom; K-12 and college]; Pyc and Rawson's .54 vs .30 says that once the item is acquired, the *harder* success is the one that pays. Third, **learner satisfaction cannot be the control signal**: Huelser and Metcalfe's participants rated the error-free condition better after a measured 20% deficit, Potts and Shanks's were "strikingly unaware" of the errorful benefit ([Potts & Shanks 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0033194)) [lab; adults], and Deslauriers's active-learning students scored 0.46 SD higher while rating their learning 0.56 SD lower ([Deslauriers et al. 2019, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6765278/)) [classroom crossover RCT; 149 Harvard students] — with a 2025 replication at 0.27 SD on learning and feeling-of-learning in the *opposite* direction ([Boedeker et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40670-024-02219-1)) [crossover; 146 medical students]. Soderstrom and Bjork's frame — "certain manipulations can have opposite effects on learning and performance" ([Soderstrom & Bjork 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000)) [review] — is the governing caveat on every lever below.

The domain split between Jansen and Papoušek deserves a direct statement because both author groups make it: Jansen's children were doing timed procedural arithmetic where fluency is the goal and volume is the mechanism, and Math Garden can hold 60–90% targets only because "response time is used as an additional source of information" ([Jansen 2013](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-influence-of-experiencing-success-in-math-on-math-anxiety-perceived-math-competence-and-math-performance-pdf-free.html)); Pelánek's users were learning declarative facts where a 95% target means two-option questions the learner already knows, and the UMUAI paper attributes the disagreement with Jansen and Lomas to "different types of knowledge components" ([Pelánek et al. 2017](http://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/umuai-adaptive-practice.pdf)). Sapling's content is mostly conceptual college material, closer to Pelánek's than to Jansen's, but with captive-session rather than voluntary use, which shifts the calculus back toward per-item learning over dose. And no human study anywhere randomizes learners across success targets in an academic tutoring domain and measures both retention and dropout; the closest, E-Gotsky, pushed first-attempt success from ~85% toward ~78% by skipping items predicted too easy, cut guessing ~25%, and got mixed quiz results ([E-Gotsky 2019, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12268)) [field trial; 4 Israeli schools].

## Set the target by phase and format, not by one number

The industry consensus and the human evidence together support a phase-specific policy: **probe near 0.5–0.62, acquire near 0.75–0.90 with scaffolds, practise unassisted near 0.65–0.85, review at R ≈ 0.90, gate mastery at ≥ 0.95 or three unassisted first-attempt successes in a row.** ALEKS assesses at ~0.5 and its learning mode's "ready to learn" fringe empirically succeeds at 0.809 (Sixth-Grade Math, 38.9M attempts) and 0.841 (Algebra I, 21.8M), with a median item success of 0.863 and any item under 0.60 treated as a mis-placed fringe ([Matayoshi et al. 2021](https://jmatayoshi.github.io/publications/JMP2021_KST_ALEKS_preprint.pdf)) [vendor; production logs]. Math Garden and outlinemaps default to 0.75 ([Klinkenberg et al. 2011](https://core.ac.uk/works/43750912); [Pelánek 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S036013151630080X)); Cognitive Tutor gates at P(known) 0.95 and selects the unmastered component closest to it ([NAP](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10297/chapter/6); [AAAI](https://cdn.aaai.org/ocs/4483/4483-21568-1-PB.pdf)) [vendor]; FSRS review at desired retention 0.90 keeps predicted recall oscillating between 100% and 90% ([fsrs4anki tutorial](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md)) [algorithm docs].

| **Mastery gate** | P(known) ≥ 0.95 proficient; ≥ 0.98 with ≥ 3 strong-channel unassisted observations mastered; stop practising when belief is stable | Route to scheduler; never over-practise | Cen 2007 12% time saved at no cost [classroom]; Beck & Gong 3-in-a-row [log] |

By learner level the modifiers are consistent across three generations of tutors. Novices gain most per item and disengage fastest (Lomas low-ability steeper curves, p = .002; D'Mello's supportive tutor helped only low-knowledge students, 0.71σ, and only in the harder second session ([D'Mello & Graesser 2012](https://www.ida.liu.se/divisions/hcs/seminars/cogsciseminars/Papers/dmello-TiiS12.pdf)) [RCT]), so they get the higher band, worked examples first, and an error-triggered *offer* of help because low-knowledge learners under-seek: in Wood and Wood's 42 algebra pupils, help-seeking relative to errors correlated with outcomes at partial r = 0.52 for the low-prior group but −0.22 for the high group, yet the high group was the one more likely to ask after an error (0.27 vs 0.11, P < 0.02), and waiting longer before asking correlated with better outcomes (r = 0.41) ([Wood & Wood 1999](https://www.tlu.ee/~kpata/haridustehnoloogiaTLU/tutoring.pdf)) [correlational; K-12]. Developing students go problem-first with hints on request. Proficient students get verification-only feedback, a lower help ceiling, shorter dialogues, and the *option* to select harder items, since moderate difficulty is motivating only when chosen (Lomas 2017) and assistance hurts them (d = −0.428).

By check format the target must be chance-corrected and effort-gated. A 4-option multiple-choice item at 0.75 observed is roughly 0.67 true knowledge; outlinemaps varies the option count from 2 to 6 precisely to hit its target ([Papoušek & Pelánek 2015](http://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/aied15.pdf)); E-Gotsky flagged responses under 2.1 s as guesses ([arXiv 1904.12268](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12268)); Math Garden scores speed and accuracy together because response time carries knowledge information for basic arithmetic. Free-response and teach-back checks have low guess rates and should run at the lower edge of each band; multiple choice at the upper edge, with a reason tier before a correct answer earns strong-channel weight. Kang's finding that short-answer practice beat multiple choice on a later MC test (d = 0.41) holds **only with feedback** — without feedback MC beat SA (d = 0.36) — so free response is the higher-yield check only when the corrective answer follows ([Kang, McDermott & Roediger 2007](http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Kang-et-al-2007_EJCP.pdf)) [lab; n = 48 per experiment].

Two dialogue-level moves deserve a place alongside the ladder because they raise success without giving the answer. And what the tutor *knows* about the learner moved independent next-item success more than any conversational tweak Khan tried: recent-history summary +3.4%, prerequisite gaps +2.7%, combined +6.1% over 15M threads, versus "no effect" for example-type changes ([Khan Academy 2026](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)) [vendor; no CIs]. ## Pushing success high fails in seven documented ways

Every failure mode below is a way of raising success by shrinking the task, and each has a number and a mitigation with a number.

| Failure mode | Evidence (tier; population) | Mitigation with measured effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Help abuse and hint skimming** | Students viewed 68% of pre-bottom-out hint levels for < 1 s; after 3 errors the next action was a hint request only 34% of the time; raw hint frequency correlates negatively with gain, time-per-hint-level positively, bottom-out dwell positively ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)) [ITS logs; K-12] | Gate the next rung on a genuine attempt and on dwell time at the current rung; make bottom-out an assisted (Hard) observation that counts zero toward the 3-in-a-row criterion; Shute's guideline 16: "avoid using progressive hints that always terminate with the correct answer" ([Shute 2007](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/shute%202007_f.pdf)) [review] |
| **Mastery-gain paradox** | A monolithic GPT-4o tutor produced higher BKT mastery gain (+0.70 vs +0.40) only by giving 12× more hints (6.8 vs 0.44); attempt-before-hint enforcement restored hint efficiency 3.3× ([ES-LLMs 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23990)) [bench; synthetic] | Optimise unassisted next-item success, never in-dialogue success or ΔP(known); this is the Khan and Tutor CoPilot metric |
| **Answer leakage** | ChatGPT-as-tutor revealed the solution in 66% of turns, Telling@10 32%; fine-tuning on human tutoring → 4% ([MathDial](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14536)) [bench]. Under adversarial students 70–83% leak; reasoning-wrapped tutor 46% → 2%, multi-agent 46% → 4% ([arXiv 2604.18660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18660)) [bench]. | **Wheel-spinning** | 45% of ASSISTments student-skill pairs unmastered after 10 problems and only 4% more by 30; wheel-spinners answer 31–36% correct ([Gong 2014](https://digital.wpi.edu/downloads/9306sz45f)) [logs]; 43.8% of non-persistent pairs quit unmastered; detector AUC 0.684 ([Kai et al. 2018](https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/download/210/88)) [log]; correct-response-percentage alone gives 93.5% precision / 77.1% recall after 4 steps ([Zhang et al. 2019](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED594575.pdf)) [log] | Detect at 4–10 opportunities; stop more-of-the-same and route to prerequisite repair ("additional tutoring interventions … should suffice to get the student unstuck") |

The guardrail evidence has a ceiling of its own that should temper expectations: Bastani's hint-not-answer arm removed the harm but produced no positive transfer either, and the positive results (Kestin's d ≈ 0.6–1.3 with pre-authored step solutions and one-part-at-a-time sequencing, n = 194 ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)) [classroom crossover RCT; no delayed test]; Khan's +6.1%) come from systems that added *content structure and learner state*, not from withholding alone.

## Measure in-ZPD-ness as hints-to-criterion and unassisted next-opportunity success

Sapling should compute four per-student, per-concept quantities and log the raw fields that produce them. **Hints-to-criterion** is the DA instrument: the mean number of ladder rungs consumed before a correct response over the last k opportunities on the concept, with the expected trajectory that it falls toward zero as the zone moves (Ferrara's stability across related tasks makes it usable as a learner-level trait too). **Unassisted next-opportunity success** is the outcome the ITS and LLM literatures both validated — Beck and Gong's mastery is "three consecutive problems correctly … on his first attempt, and requests no assistance from the tutor prior to that" ([Gong 2014](https://digital.wpi.edu/downloads/9306sz45f)); Roll's hint benefit is measured on it; Khan's production metric is "next-item correctness without assistance" — and it is the number whose disagreement with in-session success exposes help abuse and the mastery-gain paradox. **Assist gap** (assisted-minus-unassisted first-attempt success over the same window) is the literal Vygotsky quantity; a wide gap with high assisted success is "in the zone," a narrow gap at high unassisted success is "beyond it," and a wide gap at near-zero assisted success is "below it" (the comprehension bound). **Uptake** — whether the student's next turn builds on the tutor's — has a validated automatic estimator (pJSD reaches Spearman ρ = .540 with human labels at the rater ceiling and correlates with instruction quality and satisfaction across three datasets ([Demszky et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.130/)) [log; 2,246 NCTE exchanges]), but it is validated against ratings, not learning, so it is a leading indicator; real deployments show uptake of 0.30–0.68 with scaffolding and −0.02 to −0.07 without ([arXiv 2606.15766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15766)).

The secondary signals have known signs. Time per hint level is positive and raw hint count negative (Aleven 2016); time-to-first-attempt below ~2 s is a guess (E-Gotsky's 2.1 s floor); time-to-correct rising with error count on one sub-goal is the frustration signature in the affect state machine ([D'Mello & Graesser 2012](https://www.ida.liu.se/divisions/hcs/seminars/cogsciseminars/Papers/dmello-TiiS12.pdf)); an explicit perceived-difficulty prompt after ~30 items ("too easy / appropriate / too difficult") is cheap, and outlinemaps found the "appropriate" peak at ~65% achieved success with 30% error as the felt optimum ([FIMU-RS-2016-02](https://www.fi.muni.cz/reports/files/2016/FIMU-RS-2016-02.pdf)). Wheel-spinning detectors need only correct-response count, correct-in-a-row count, an exponential-mean response-time z-score and hint-request counts (Gong 2014, §7). What must **not** be the control signal is felt learning or satisfaction, for the metacognitive reasons above, and what must not be the objective is ΔP(known), which a hint-heavy tutor inflates. The evaluation ladder from the earlier report stands: offline leakage and sycophancy fixtures, online A/B on unassisted next-item success and first-attempt correctness on the next scheduled review, a tool-removed unit post-test, and delayed retention — with within-student randomization of concepts to policy variants so that Sapling's tens-of-students-per-course regime can detect effects the way Lindsey's 179 students detected d = 1.4.

Four earlier recommendations change as a result. **The turn-shape rule softens.** The earlier report justified "learner-paced, never auto-advance" with Rey's 0.45 vs 0.35; the corrected table says learner pacing wins on *transfer* while system pacing wins on *retention* (0.42 vs 0.19 n.s.), and retention benefits are largest for learners with high prior knowledge (0.73) and absent for those with some (−0.12). Sapling should keep the student-ended turn for conceptual and transfer steps and let the tutor set cadence for fact and fluency drill and spaced review. **The probe spec loses its psychometric footing.** "4–6 items" came from a depression-scale simulation; keep it as a heuristic, tier it as [simulation; adult patient-reported outcome], move the probe target from "50–70%" to **0.50–0.62** on the PISA/Ortner affect evidence, and stop on belief stability or the floor rule rather than a nominal SE. **The 75–90% band is re-justified and split.** It was grounded on Wilson; it now rests on ALEKS's observed fringe success (0.81–0.86), Math Garden's 0.75, and Metcalfe's easiest-unlearned rule for *assisted acquisition*, while *unassisted practice* moves down to 65–85% on Papoušek and the motor tests, and both are explicitly domain-conditional. **Free response is conditional on feedback**, and Chi's "prompting is as good as explaining" is downgraded to an n = 11 null, so the novice step keeps its explanation and the Socratic-only default stays off. Two smaller adjustments: Slavin's mastery-learning medians (0.225–0.31) replace "~0.25," and "generating teaching materials" (g = 0.17) is a weak lever that should not be promoted to a teach-back substitute.

## Sapling ZPD policy spec

The spec below assumes the per-channel BKT learner model, FSRS-6 scheduler, and graded-check tool proposed in the earlier report; it adds the ZPD layer on top. Everything is computed in Sapling code from trusted learner state, never from student text, and the LLM only delivers the rung the policy selects. Thresholds marked † are engineering choices with no validated cut-point in any source and should be treated as the first A/B candidates.

```
STATE (per user × concept)
  p_known            BKT posterior, decayed to prior at read (earlier report)
  band               novice  : p_known < 0.30
                     develop : 0.30 ≤ p_known < 0.80
                     profic  : p_known ≥ 0.80
  htc_k              mean hint rungs consumed before correct, last k=5 opportunities
  unassisted_next    first-attempt correct with zero hints, last k=8 opportunities
  assist_gap         assisted_first_attempt_rate − unassisted_first_attempt_rate, last 8
  streak_unassisted  consecutive unassisted first-attempt correct (mastery needs 3)
  opps               opportunities on this concept since first teach
  wheelspin          opps ≥ 10 AND streak_unassisted never reached 3
                     OR (opps ≥ 6 AND unassisted_next < 0.50)

LADDER (rung → what the tutor may emit)
  H0 acknowledge / verification feedback on a correct step
  H1 pump or focus question ("what is the first thing you need?")
  H2 concept pointer: retrieved course passage or definition, cited
  H3 leading question about the very next step
  H4 worked example on an ISOMORPH (different surface, same structure)
  H5 completion problem: partial solution, last steps blanked (backward fade)
  H6 full solution — practice items only, never graded coursework, never exam mode

CEILING (max rung this turn), from state only
  exam_mode                              → H0–H1
  band = profic                          → H1 (H3 after 2 failed genuine attempts)
  band = develop                         → H3; +1 rung per failed genuine attempt; H6 after ≥ 2
  band = novice, prerequisite proficient → attempt-first, ceiling H5
  band = novice, prerequisite not known  → worked example FIRST (H4), then H5, then near-transfer
  student shows own work / buggy step    → floor at H3 (never below), so debugging help is allowed

GATES
  genuine attempt   = a submitted answer or shown work, not "idk"/"just tell me", after
                      ≥ 45 s† (novice ≥ 90 s†) of independent time on the step
  next rung         requires a genuine attempt since the last rung AND dwell ≥ 8 s† on it
  H6                requires ≥ 2 genuine attempts, item.taught = true, item.graded = false
  offer (not push)  after a wrong genuine attempt for novice band only: "want a hint?"
  student choice    may opt into "harder" (raises item difficulty one step); may not lower
                    the ceiling floor or skip attempts

SUCCESS BANDS (rolling 8 items, first attempt)
  probe             expected P(correct) 0.50–0.62; 4–6 items/skill, cap 8–12;
                    stop on 3 misses at easiest level → teach lowest prerequisite
  acquisition       0.75–0.90 after scaffold (assisted)
  practice          0.65–0.85 unassisted
  review            FSRS desired retention 0.90 (0.85 large sets; 0.95 exam week)
  peer/ConcepTest   ask the room only when 35–70% of ≥ 5 members are initially correct

BAND CONTROL
  unassisted_next > 0.90 for 2 windows AND p_known ≥ 0.95 AND belief stable
                                        → stop practice, hand to scheduler (over-practice)
  unassisted_next > 0.90, p_known < 0.95 → raise item difficulty ONE step; drop ceiling 1 rung
  0.65 ≤ unassisted_next ≤ 0.90          → hold
  unassisted_next < 0.65                 → check prerequisites; lower difficulty freely;
                                           raise ceiling 1 rung
  wheelspin = true                       → STOP more-of-the-same; prerequisite repair;
                                           flag for human/instructor; emit zpd.wheelspin

EVIDENCE MAPPING (into BKT / FSRS)
  unassisted first-attempt correct  → full strong-channel observation; FSRS Good
  correct after H1–H3               → assisted observation (halve weight†); FSRS Hard;
                                      counts 0 toward streak_unassisted
  correct after H4–H6               → no upward BKT evidence; FSRS Again; re-ask isomorph
                                      next session unassisted
  wrong, any rung                   → standard incorrect observation; corrective feedback
                                      WITH the answer, immediately, never delayed
  same-session re-check             → half weight (earlier report)
  confidence / reason tier          → diagnosis only (misconception vs ignorance,
                                      hypercorrection target); never a hint gate

LOG (append-only; event names follow the quiz.* convention)
  zpd.step      request_id, user_id, concept_id, question_hash, phase, channel, band,
                ceiling, ceiling_reason, first_attempt_correct, n_attempts,
                max_rung_used, rungs[{rung, dwell_ms}], time_to_first_attempt_ms,
                time_to_correct_ms, independent_time_ms, assisted, confidence,
                fsrs_rating, p_known_before, p_known_after, R_before, item_difficulty
  zpd.offer     accepted/declined, band
  zpd.band_adjust  direction, trigger, window_stats
  zpd.wheelspin concept_id, opps, unassisted_next, htc_k, prerequisite_ids
  zpd.leak      judge verdict per risky turn (rung emitted vs ceiling)
  zpd.rating    perceived difficulty after every 30 checks: too_easy/appropriate/too_hard
  session       survival (checks completed), return_within_7d
  derived nightly per user×concept: htc_k, unassisted_next, assist_gap, in_zone flag
                (assist_gap ≥ 0.25 AND assisted_rate ≥ 0.6 AND unassisted_next < 0.85)
```

The three headline KPIs are the share of steps whose rolling unassisted success sits inside the phase band, the trend of hints-to-criterion per concept (it must fall as the concept matures; a flat or rising htc_k with rising in-session success is the mastery-gain paradox in the wild), and unassisted next-opportunity success on the following session's first item for the concept, which is the number the A/B tests optimise. Leakage and sycophancy rates from the judge are gates, not KPIs: the deployed supervisor architecture's gates of zero reveals, ≤ 5% earnest-revise and ≥ 95% ceiling compliance are the bar ([arXiv 2608.12292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12292)).

## Open questions the evidence cannot settle

The central empirical question of this brief is untested: no study measures whether a scaffold that raises first-attempt success from ~0.6 to ~0.8 preserves the per-item learning of the harder version, in any domain. No human study randomizes learners across success targets in academic tutoring content and measures both retention and dropout; the two that come closest disagree by knowledge type, and Sapling's conceptual college material is a third type. The 45 s / 90 s independent-time gate and the 8 s dwell gate have no validated cut-points — van de Pol 2015 shows the *direction* of the independent-time effect and Aleven 2016 shows that sub-second hint views are useless, and that is all. No LLM-era ladder publishes a mastery or attempt threshold tied to a learning outcome; ES-LLM's p_L > 0.95 and "attempt count = 0" are design constants. No LLM tutor study reports a delayed, tool-removed test with per-move attribution, so the entire dialogue-level lever set (verification feedback, probing, one question per turn) is validated against next-turn productivity or rater preference, not learning. Confidence-gated hinting has never been tested for learning. The chance correction for multiple choice and the guess-latency floor have one source each. Whether fluency drill on facts in Sapling (flashcards) should run at Jansen's easier targets while conceptual practice runs at Pelánek's harder ones is a hypothesis the log fields above are designed to test. And three primary texts the theory rests on — Chaiklin 2003, Wood, Bruner and Ross 1976, and Wood, Wood and Middleton 1978 — were reachable only through summaries; nothing in the policy depends on their unverified details, but the "contingent shift" rule should be cited as Wood & Wood 1999 until the 1978 numbers are read.

## Conclusion

The re-read of the primary sources changes what "optimize for ZPD" means for Sapling. Vygotsky gives a measurement, not a target; the measurement — how much graded help closes the gap between alone and assisted, and whether the gap closes on the next unassisted try — is exactly what a hint ladder with attempt gating produces as a by-product of tutoring. Once that is the objective, the success-rate debate resolves itself: the number a student sees on screen is allowed to be high during acquisition because scaffolds that raise the learner (prerequisites, faded examples, sub-goals, warm-ups) carry no documented learning cost, and it must be allowed to fall to 65–85% during unassisted practice and review because effortful success is what the retention data reward. The one thing the system must never do is buy success by shrinking the task, and the log fields that expose that purchase — hints-to-criterion rising while in-session accuracy rises, assisted and unassisted rates diverging — are cheaper to compute than the mastery score Sapling moves today by the tutor's own judgment.

The second implication is about who owns the dial. Every failure mode with a number behind it — help abuse, leakage, sycophancy, the mastery-gain paradox — is the language model optimising the wrong quantity, and every mitigation with a number behind it is a deterministic layer outside the model holding the ceiling, gating on attempts, and grounding in a verified answer. Sapling already holds the course material, the syllabus and the student's notes; adding a policy layer that computes a help ceiling from `p_known`, counts rungs, and demands an unassisted re-check turns the ZPD from a metaphor in the prompt into a measured state in the database, which is the only form in which it can be optimised at all.
